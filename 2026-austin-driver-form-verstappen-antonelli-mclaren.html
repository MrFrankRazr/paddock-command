Austin Driver Form Watch: Verstappen’s Surge, Antonelli’s Title Cushion

The championship leader is 102 points clear, but the hottest driver on recent Grand Prix form is Max Verstappen. Before Austin, the three-race results tell a different story from the season standings.

Three Grands Prix that changed the form picture

George Russell won in Baku on September 26, but Red Bull's trajectory turned decisively at the next two rounds. Verstappen took victory at Sepang on October 4 and followed it with a dominant win in Singapore on October 11. That makes two consecutive Grand Prix wins after a runner-up finish in Azerbaijan, plus a Singapore Sprint victory.

Antonelli has not won any of those three Grands Prix, yet P5, P2 and P3 have protected his title advantage. Russell's Baku victory was followed by a late power-unit retirement at Sepang and a ninth-place finish in Singapore. The contrast between raw recent form and the championship table is central to the Austin story.

Driver-by-driver intelligence

Verstappen: the momentum benchmark

His P2–P1–P1 sequence is worth 68 Grand Prix points, 25 more than Antonelli across the same three races. COTA's heavy braking and fast direction changes offer another test of whether Red Bull's resurgence transfers across circuit types. The team also faces a race-engineer handover for Austin.

Antonelli: title management, not desperation

Two consecutive podiums have put the Mercedes driver on 340 points, 102 clear of Russell. The race-weekend objective is to maximize points without letting the pressure of a possible title clinch create avoidable risk. Exact clinching scenarios depend on every contender's result.

Russell: urgency after two setbacks

Russell won Baku but scored only two Grand Prix points in the next two races combined. Sepang's power-unit failure and Singapore's grid recovery left him 102 points behind. Austin presents an opportunity to reassert Mercedes' pace and keep the title fight alive.

Leclerc: the consistency threat

Leclerc's P4–P4–P2 sequence is worth 42 Grand Prix points, just one fewer than Antonelli's total. The Singapore runner-up finish suggests Ferrari should not be dismissed in the fight for a front-row start or Sunday podium.

Norris and Piastri: McLaren's narrow margin

Norris took P4 and Piastri P5 in Singapore, but McLaren holds only a seven-point constructors' advantage over Red Bull. Norris has described the MCL40 as unusually demanding to drive; Austin will test whether the car can deliver consistent qualifying laps and race pace.

Hadjar: points that matter to Red Bull

Hadjar's Baku podium and Sepang P5 provided valuable support, although Singapore yielded only P10. If Red Bull is to move ahead of McLaren in the constructors' standings, its second car remains a significant variable.

Championship Watch: two races within the race

Official standings entering Austin: Antonelli 340, Russell 238, Hamilton 229, Verstappen 221, Leclerc 215 and Norris 201. Mercedes leads the constructors' standings with 578 points, ahead of Ferrari on 444; McLaren's 339 and Red Bull's 332 create a particularly tight battle for third.

The title calculation and the fight for third in the constructors' standings should be covered separately. A Verstappen victory can continue Red Bull's rise without necessarily preventing Antonelli from securing the drivers' title, depending on the finishing positions of Russell and other challengers.

What Austin practice and qualifying must answer

  1. Is Red Bull quick in both high-speed direction changes and slow-corner exits? COTA's Esses and long back straight test different qualities from Singapore.
  2. Can Mercedes deliver a clean weekend for both cars? Antonelli's points position is strong, but Russell needs a result after Sepang and Singapore.
  3. Does McLaren's handling limitation translate into qualifying errors or race tyre degradation? Watch both drivers, not just Norris.
  4. Can Ferrari convert Leclerc's consistent form into a sustained challenge? Friday long runs will provide better evidence than predictions made before the cars run.
