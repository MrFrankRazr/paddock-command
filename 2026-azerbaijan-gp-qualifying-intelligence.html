QUALIFYING INTELLIGENCE · BAKU · ROUND 15

Russell Takes Baku Pole as Antonelli Crash Opens Championship Opportunity

Russell claimed pole by 0.837 seconds over Leclerc, with Piastri third and returning Hadjar fourth. Championship leader Antonelli crashed in Q1 and qualified 16th.

Paddock Command editorial · Published Sep 25, 2026
Azerbaijan GP · Qualifying complete · Race Saturday

FRONT ROWRussell · Leclerc

Russell turns Thursday control into a decisive pole

George Russell followed his Thursday FP1 and FP2 sweep with pole for the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix. His 1:42.526 was 0.837 seconds faster than Charles Leclerc, a substantial qualifying margin on a weekend when several teams appeared competitive. Russell now has track position for Saturday, but Baku’s long straight and frequent race interruptions mean the opening stint still demands execution.

Antonelli’s Q1 crash changes the championship equation

Championship leader Kimi Antonelli hit the inside wall at Turn 1 during Q1, damaging the front-left of his Mercedes. He did not progress beyond the first qualifying segment and was classified 16th. Entering Baku, Antonelli held 292 points to Russell’s 211, an 81-point lead. Qualifying does not alter that margin; Saturday’s race will determine whether Russell can reduce it.

Leclerc and Piastri line up behind Russell

Charles Leclerc qualified second in 1:43.363, with Oscar Piastri only 0.001 seconds slower in third. Piastri’s front-two-row start follows a disrupted Thursday for McLaren and makes the opening laps particularly important. Lando Norris qualified fifth, with Lewis Hamilton sixth.

Hadjar’s return produces a fourth-place qualifying result

Isack Hadjar qualified fourth with a 1:43.500 on his first race weekend back after a three-Grand-Prix absence caused by a wrist injury. That result moves Paddock Command’s Red Bull watch from physical readiness alone to race execution: a clean launch, braking consistency and completing 51 laps will be the meaningful tests.

Verstappen’s FP3 pace did not carry into Q3

Max Verstappen topped final practice ahead of Russell, but qualified eighth. The difference between FP3 pace and the grid leaves Red Bull with two distinct race strategies: Hadjar starts among the leading contenders, while Verstappen faces traffic and recovery work.

Williams finds a qualifying signal from its upgrade

Carlos Sainz reached Q3 and qualified ninth as Williams evaluated its major Baku development package. One qualifying result is not a complete measure of the FW48’s progress; long-run pace and the ability to compete for points will provide further evidence on Saturday.

Saturday race intelligence: five watch points

• Russell’s launch and first-lap positioning against Leclerc and Piastri.

• Antonelli’s recovery from 16th without compromising the car on Baku’s narrow sections.

• Hadjar’s race-distance consistency after missing three events.

• Verstappen’s ability to recover from eighth, especially around pit-stop timing.

• Safety Car timing, tyre management and the long run toward Turn 1.

When to watch

The Azerbaijan Grand Prix starts Saturday, September 26 at 15:00 Baku local time (UTC+4) and is scheduled for 51 laps around the 6.003 km Baku City Circuit. The provisional qualifying classification should be checked for any subsequent steward decisions or grid penalties before lights out.
