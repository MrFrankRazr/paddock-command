Russell Sweeps Baku Practice as Mercedes Sets the Early Benchmark

George Russell led both sessions on Baku's opening day. Mercedes finished FP2 first and second despite Kimi Antonelli's earlier hydraulic failure, while Verstappen, McLaren and Hadjar enter Friday with different questions to answer.

Russell sets the benchmark, but practice is not qualifying

George Russell topped both Thursday sessions at the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix. His FP1 lap of 1:45.387 put him 0.400 seconds ahead of Max Verstappen, with Charles Leclerc third. Russell improved to 1:43.347 in FP2 and led Mercedes team-mate Kimi Antonelli by 0.552 seconds. Verstappen finished FP2 third, 0.827 seconds behind Russell.

Mercedes has established the strongest opening-day result. Fuel loads, tyre choices and run plans still complicate direct comparisons, so Friday will determine how much of that advantage survives qualifying preparation.

Antonelli recovers after a costly FP1 interruption

Championship leader Antonelli completed only nine laps in FP1 before a hydraulic issue stopped his Mercedes at Turn 7. Mercedes changed his engine before FP2, delaying his second-session programme, but he recovered to second place. Russell's sweep and Antonelli's recovery provide two different Mercedes storylines: immediate speed and lost preparation time.

Verstappen stays in the conversation

Verstappen finished second in FP1 and third in FP2. His FP2 deficit leaves work for Red Bull before qualifying, but the Thursday order does not yet settle race pace or Friday's single-lap picture.

McLaren's upgrade test loses clean running

McLaren entered Baku with significant aerodynamic development to evaluate. Lando Norris completed only 14 laps in FP1 after movement in the floor area required a temporary repair; he ended that session 14th. Oscar Piastri also spent time in the garage while his car was checked. Those interruptions make it premature to conclude whether the new package has delivered the expected gain.

Hadjar's comeback faces another interruption

Isack Hadjar returned to Red Bull after missing three races with a wrist injury, but reported braking difficulty and then lost running to an engine-related issue. Eight FP1 laps and 20th place were not the representative comeback programme Red Bull wanted. Friday's remaining practice will be important for rebuilding confidence and preparation before qualifying.

Baku punishes mistakes before the grid is set

Arvid Lindblad's incident at the narrow castle section brought out a red flag in FP2. The interruption reinforced how quickly a Baku session can change, particularly when teams are trying to gather data on an evolving street circuit.

Paddock Command watch pointFriday's key comparison is not Thursday's fastest lap alone. Watch Mercedes' qualifying simulations, Verstappen's response, McLaren's recovered mileage and Hadjar's ability to complete a consistent FP3 programme.

What comes next

FP3 begins Friday at 12:30 Baku local time and qualifying follows at 16:00. The 51-lap Azerbaijan Grand Prix takes place Saturday at 15:00 local time. Paddock Command's next major report will compare Friday's qualifying order with the benchmarks established today.
