CHAMPIONSHIP WATCH · AFTER ROUND 14

Antonelli Leaves Madrid 81 Points Clear as Russell Concedes Title Fight

Back-to-back victories at Monza and Madrid have moved Kimi Antonelli to 292 points, opened an 81-point championship advantage and prompted George Russell to acknowledge that his own drivers' title challenge is no longer realistic.

Paddock Command editorial · Updated Sep 13, 2026

Madrid changes more than the points gap

Antonelli arrived at Madring 66 points ahead of Russell. He leaves 81 clear after winning Formula 1's first race at the circuit while Russell finished fifth.

Antonelli now has 292 points to Russell's 211. Lewis Hamilton remains third on 191 despite retiring in Madrid with a brake problem, Lando Norris is fourth on 186, Charles Leclerc has 167 and Max Verstappen 145.

The numerical change is significant. The post-race reaction makes it more important.

Russell no longer views himself as a realistic title challenger

After Madrid, Russell publicly acknowledged that he no longer considers himself realistically in contention for the Drivers' Championship.

That represents a meaningful change in the championship picture. Russell remains second in the standings, but the strategic objective is beginning to shift from chasing Antonelli across the season toward maximizing individual weekends and helping Mercedes secure the Constructors' Championship.

Madrid explains why. Russell finished fifth and scored 10 points. Antonelli won and scored 25. A deficit that was already substantial increased by another 15 points in a single race.

Consistency alone is no longer sufficient. To materially change the trajectory, Russell needs victories while Antonelli has weekends where he gives significant points back.

Antonelli's advantage is now performance plus margin

Antonelli's last five Grand Prix finishes are P1, P3, P2, P1 and P1.

That sequence contains three victories and four podiums, with the two most recent wins coming at very different circuits and under very different circumstances.

Monza required recovery from 19th on the grid. Madrid required Antonelli to remain close enough to exploit the decisive Virtual Safety Car pit window.

Antonelli acknowledged after the race that the Madrid victory benefited from fortunate VSC timing and that Norris had controlled the race beforehand. Championship campaigns, however, are also defined by converting opportunities when they appear. Antonelli did exactly that.

Russell's Madrid result illustrates the problem

Russell's Madrid race was not a collapse. The problem is increasingly not poor performance; it is that Antonelli continues converting opportunities into victories while Russell is converting competitive weekends into smaller points totals.

An 81-point deficit does not require marginal improvement. It requires a sustained reversal in results.

Hamilton's retirement compresses the fight behind second

Hamilton remains third on 191 following his brake-related retirement, but Norris has moved to within five points on 186.

Leclerc sits on 167 and Verstappen on 145, giving Madrid two different championship stories: Antonelli is separating himself from the front of the chase while the group behind Russell is becoming more competitive.

Norris in particular remains important. His Madrid pole, early race control and recent victories underline that McLaren retains the pace to influence the championship even if Antonelli's points advantage is becoming difficult to attack directly.

Mercedes enters the decisive stretch from a position of control

Baku begins an intense phase of the season. The Azerbaijan weekend starts the next run of races with Antonelli holding significantly more strategic tolerance than his rivals.

Russell and the chasing group increasingly need to attack weekends. Antonelli can continue prioritizing high-value finishes while taking victories when opportunities emerge.

Championship signal
Antonelli: 292 · Russell: 211 · Hamilton: 191 · Norris: 186 · Leclerc: 167 · Verstappen: 145.
Antonelli's lead is 81 points, up 15 from the start of Madrid.

Baku becomes the first test of the new reality

Formula 1 returns at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix from September 24–26.

Baku provides a completely different challenge from Madring: long straights, heavy braking, low-speed technical sections and a street-circuit environment where small errors can have large consequences.

For Russell, Norris, Hamilton and the rest of the chasing group, the requirement is increasingly clear. They no longer simply need strong races. They need to start taking meaningful points out of Antonelli.

Madrid ended with an 81-point lead. It also ended with Antonelli's closest rival openly acknowledging how difficult that gap has become to overcome.
