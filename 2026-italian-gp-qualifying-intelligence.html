Gasly Stuns Monza With Maiden Formula 1 Pole

Pierre Gasly overturned the Monza form book with a 1:21.786 to beat George Russell by 0.060 seconds and claim the first Formula 1 pole position of his career.

Updated Sep 5, 2026 · Post-qualifying grid penalties confirmed

Pierre Gasly has overturned the Monza form book — and a post-qualifying penalty has reshaped the grid behind him.

After a weekend dominated by Mercedes and Ferrari, the Alpine driver produced the lap of qualifying to claim the first Formula 1 pole position of his career, beating George Russell by just 0.060 seconds.

Gasly’s 1:21.786 remains the benchmark, but Oscar Piastri will no longer start third. The McLaren driver has received a three-place grid penalty for impeding Liam Lawson during Q2.

That promotes Charles Leclerc to third, Lewis Hamilton to fourth and Max Verstappen to fifth, creating an all-Ferrari second row at Monza.

Piastri drops to sixth.

Kimi Antonelli, Alex Albon and Liam Lawson are all set to start from the rear because of power-unit penalties.

Gasly keeps the headline

The grid changes do not alter the biggest Saturday story: Gasly is on pole.

His 1:21.786 lap remains 0.060 seconds faster than Russell’s best effort and gives Alpine control of the first grid slot for Sunday’s Italian Grand Prix.

For Gasly, it is the first pole position of his Formula 1 career and another landmark Monza result after his breakthrough victory at the circuit in 2020.

Russell retains the prime championship opportunity

Russell remains alongside Gasly on the front row and still owns the strongest starting position among Antonelli’s direct championship challengers.

Mercedes had led both FP2 and FP3 before qualifying, and Russell’s underlying race pace remains one of the central Sunday variables.

With Antonelli sent to the rear by his power-unit penalties, Russell has a rare opportunity to take a meaningful bite out of the championship leader’s 59-point advantage.

Piastri loses third after Lawson incident

Oscar Piastri initially delivered McLaren’s strongest qualifying result of the weekend with the third-fastest time.

He will not start there.

The stewards handed Piastri a three-place grid penalty after determining that he impeded Liam Lawson during Q2. The penalty drops Piastri from third to sixth on the starting grid.

The sanction substantially changes McLaren’s strategic picture. Instead of starting immediately behind Gasly and Russell, Piastri now has both Ferraris and Verstappen between himself and the front row.

For McLaren, that makes an already difficult weekend harder. Lando Norris also starts deeper in the order, leaving the team without the front-row-adjacent track position it appeared to have immediately after qualifying.

Ferrari inherits the second row

Ferrari’s starting position has improved without either car turning another lap.

Piastri’s penalty promotes Charles Leclerc from fourth to third and Lewis Hamilton from fifth to fourth, giving Ferrari an all-Scuderia second row for its home Grand Prix.

That transforms Ferrari’s Sunday opportunity.

Gasly and Russell still control the front row, but Leclerc and Hamilton now start directly behind them with both cars positioned to influence the opening phase of the race.

Ferrari had already shown competitive long-run pace during Friday practice. The revised grid gives the team significantly better strategic leverage than the raw qualifying classification suggested.

Verstappen moves into the top five

Max Verstappen is promoted from sixth to fifth following Piastri’s penalty.

Red Bull has not displayed the outright qualifying speed of Mercedes or Ferrari this weekend, but fifth keeps Verstappen immediately connected to the leading group.

That matters because Red Bull’s Friday assessment suggested its relative strength may be better over longer runs than on a single qualifying lap.

With Gasly, Russell and both Ferraris ahead, Verstappen now has a realistic opportunity to influence the podium fight from the opening stint.

Three cars are headed to the rear

The back of the Monza grid has also become clearer.

Championship leader Kimi Antonelli, Williams driver Alex Albon and Red Bull’s Liam Lawson are all set to start from the rear after taking power-unit components beyond their permitted allocations.

Antonelli’s penalty remains the biggest championship variable.

He enters Sunday with a 59-point advantage over Russell and Hamilton, but both of his closest challengers now occupy the first two rows while he begins the race in traffic.

Russell starts second. Hamilton starts fourth. Antonelli starts at the rear.

That is the championship equation Monza will carry into Sunday.

Qualifying verdict: Ferrari just became a much bigger factor

Gasly’s maiden pole remains the headline.

The post-qualifying grid reshuffle changes almost everything immediately behind him.

Russell retains the best starting position of the championship contenders. Ferrari now controls the entire second row. Verstappen moves into fifth. Piastri falls to sixth. Antonelli faces a recovery race from the rear.

What appeared immediately after qualifying to be a Gasly-Russell-Piastri opening battle now has Ferrari inserted directly into the center of it.

Gasly must defend his first pole. Russell has an opportunity to attack Antonelli’s championship advantage. Leclerc and Hamilton have two Ferrari cars positioned together at their home race. Verstappen remains close enough to exploit any disruption.

Behind them, Piastri must recover the positions lost through his penalty.

The Italian Grand Prix was already unpredictable after Gasly’s qualifying upset. The stewards have made Sunday even more volatile.
