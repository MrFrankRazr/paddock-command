Gasly Stuns Monza With Maiden Formula 1 Pole

Pierre Gasly overturned the Monza form book with a 1:21.786 to beat George Russell by 0.060 seconds and claim the first Formula 1 pole position of his career.

Published Sep 5, 2026 · Qualifying complete

Pierre Gasly has overturned the Monza form book.

After a weekend dominated by Mercedes and Ferrari, the Alpine driver produced the lap of qualifying to claim the first Formula 1 pole position of his career, beating George Russell by just 0.060 seconds.

Gasly’s 1:21.786 put Alpine at the front of the Italian Grand Prix grid. Oscar Piastri qualified third for McLaren, Charles Leclerc fourth, Lewis Hamilton fifth and Max Verstappen sixth.

Kimi Antonelli qualified seventh but is set to start from the rear because of his power-unit penalties.

Gasly executes when it matters

The margin was tiny, but the execution was decisive.

Gasly found the lap when the circuit was at its most competitive and converted Alpine’s qualifying opportunity into an unexpected pole. Russell came within six hundredths, but Mercedes could not convert its Friday and FP3 speed into the top starting position.

For Gasly, the result is a landmark: his first Formula 1 pole and an enormous result for Alpine at a circuit where straight-line efficiency and energy deployment have dominated the weekend.

Russell misses pole after leading the weekend

Russell enters Sunday from the front row, but qualifying will still feel like an opportunity lost.

Mercedes had led FP2 and FP3, making Russell the obvious benchmark before qualifying. Antonelli’s grid penalty also gave Mercedes strategic flexibility because the championship leader’s own starting position was already compromised.

The result leaves Mercedes with strong underlying race pace but without control of the start.

Piastri salvages McLaren’s weekend

McLaren’s Friday concerns did not disappear, but Oscar Piastri delivered third place and gave the team its strongest starting position of the weekend.

Lando Norris qualified ninth on the road, leaving Piastri with a significant track-position advantage for Sunday. With Antonelli dropping back, Norris is promoted one place on the provisional grid.

That creates an important opportunity for Piastri to score heavily while the championship leader starts deep in the field.

Ferrari is close — but not close enough

Ferrari will start its home Grand Prix with Leclerc fourth and Hamilton fifth in the qualifying classification.

The SF-26 remained competitive through Saturday, but Ferrari missed the final margin needed to challenge Gasly and Russell for the front row. The team’s improved power-unit package has kept it in the fight, but Monza continues to expose how decisive execution, slipstream and end-of-straight performance can be.

Verstappen remains within striking distance

Max Verstappen qualified sixth after a weekend in which Red Bull has struggled to find a clean balance.

That leaves him behind Alpine, Mercedes, McLaren and Ferrari in the qualifying order, but close enough to influence the podium battle if Red Bull’s longer-run performance proves stronger than its single-lap pace.

Antonelli’s real race starts from the back

Antonelli qualified seventh on merit, but the position is largely irrelevant because his power-unit penalties send him to the rear.

That transforms the championship leader’s Sunday into a damage-limitation exercise. Mercedes has shown enough pace all weekend to make a recovery possible, but Gasly, Russell, Piastri, both Ferraris and Verstappen all have strong track position.

Antonelli entered Monza with a 59-point championship lead. The central question on Sunday is how much of that advantage he can protect while fighting forward.

The provisional grid reshapes the race

Antonelli is not the only driver carrying a heavy engine-related penalty. Alex Albon is also required to start from the rear after exceeding his power-unit allocation.

With Antonelli dropping out of the top seven, Franco Colapinto is promoted to seventh, Norris to eighth, Arvid Lindblad to ninth and Gabriel Bortoleto into the top 10 on the provisional grid.

The order remains subject to final FIA confirmation before the race.

Qualifying verdict: Sunday just became far less predictable

Friday suggested a Mercedes-Ferrari duel. Saturday produced Pierre Gasly on pole.

That single result changes the structure of the Italian Grand Prix. Gasly now controls the start. Russell has Mercedes pace but must attack from second. Piastri has unexpectedly strong track position. Ferrari has two cars inside the top five. Verstappen remains close enough to influence the podium battle.

Behind them, Antonelli faces one of the most important recovery races of his championship campaign.

Monza has already delivered its first major upset. Sunday now has the potential to deliver another.
