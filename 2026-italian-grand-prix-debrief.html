POST-RACE DEBRIEF · ITALIAN GRAND PRIX

Antonelli From 19th to Victory: What Monza Changed

Kimi Antonelli turned a back-of-grid penalty into a historic home victory, beat George Russell in a late Mercedes duel and extended his championship lead to 66 points.

Paddock Command editorial · Published Sep 6, 2026
Final race classification · Monza
WINNERAntonelli · P19 → P1
MERCEDES1–2 finish
TITLE LEAD66 points
NEXTMadrid · Sep 11–13

Monza was supposed to expose the championship leader

Kimi Antonelli arrived at Monza facing what looked like a damage-limitation weekend. He leaves Italy having strengthened his grip on the 2026 Formula 1 World Championship.

Starting 19th after a power-unit penalty, Antonelli charged through the field and passed Mercedes team-mate George Russell late in the Italian Grand Prix to win. Russell finished 3.857 seconds behind to complete a Mercedes one-two, with Max Verstappen third.

Antonelli became the first Italian driver to win the Italian Grand Prix since Ludovico Scarfiotti in 1966 and converted one of the championship's most vulnerable weekends into another extension of his advantage.

From the back to the front

Antonelli's Sunday began from 19th, leaving Russell, Lewis Hamilton and the other championship challengers with an obvious opportunity to reduce his lead.

Charles Leclerc's race ended on Lap 2 after a heavy crash at Parabolica triggered a red flag for barrier repairs. Antonelli continued working through the field after the restart and the closing phase became a Mercedes fight.

With fresher tyres, Antonelli completed the decisive move on Russell with four laps remaining and pulled clear.

Russell's championship opportunity disappeared

Russell started second while Antonelli started 19th, with 59 points separating them. Russell scored 18 points for second; Antonelli scored 25 for the win. The championship lead increased to 66 points, with Antonelli on 267 and Russell on 201.

Ferrari's home opportunity collapsed

Ferrari entered Sunday with Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton on the second row after Oscar Piastri's post-qualifying penalty. Leclerc retired after his early crash and Hamilton finished sixth.

Mercedes' one-two widened the constructors' picture: Mercedes leaves Monza on 468 points, Ferrari on 346, McLaren on 287 and Red Bull on 204.

Antonelli now has both momentum and control

Across the last five Grands Prix, Antonelli's finishing sequence is P16, P1, P3, P2 and P1. Russell's is P2, retirement, P7, P3 and P2. Norris' is P4, P7, P1, P1 and P4.

The championship leader is no longer simply protecting a large points cushion. He is also producing the strongest combination of recent podium consistency and championship conversion.

What Monza changed

Antonelli leads Russell by 66 points. Hamilton is third on 191, Norris fourth on 171, Leclerc fifth on 155 and Verstappen sixth on 127.

Monza was the weekend most likely to expose Antonelli. Instead, he won. That raises the burden on every challenger heading into the next phase of the season.

Madrid comes immediately

Formula 1 now heads directly to Madrid for the inaugural Spanish Grand Prix at Madring from September 11–13. A new circuit gives every team a fresh engineering problem and creates the next opportunity for the competitive order to move.
