The championship arrives at Monza with room to move

Andrea Kimi Antonelli leaves Zandvoort on 242 points, 59 clear of George Russell and Lewis Hamilton, who are level on 183. Lando Norris is fourth on 159 after winning the Dutch Grand Prix. That gives Antonelli a meaningful cushion, but Monza begins a stretch in which one retirement or one badly timed strategy call can change the feel of the championship quickly.

The key distinction is between lead size and control. Antonelli does not need to win every race. His priority is to keep banking heavy points while rivals need bigger swings. Norris, Russell and Hamilton have more incentive to attack because merely matching the leader does not materially change the title picture.

Paddock Command watch pointWatch the gap after qualifying as much as the grid order. At Monza, tow effects and tiny lap-time differences can compress the field, making track position less stable than a normal starting order suggests.

Why Monza changes the engineering conversation

Monza is a 5.793 km circuit built around long full-throttle sections, heavy braking and low-downforce efficiency. The race runs for 53 laps over 306.72 km. That combination shifts the trade-off away from maximum cornering load and toward straight-line efficiency, braking stability and traction out of the chicanes.

A car that looked excellent through Zandvoort's loaded corners may not automatically carry that advantage to Italy. Monza rewards a different aerodynamic compromise. Teams must find enough stability to attack the braking zones without carrying drag that makes them vulnerable on the straights.

Three questions before Friday practice

1. Can McLaren turn Norris's momentum into another front-row fight?

Norris has won the last two Grands Prix on the calendar. Monza will test whether that form travels to a circuit with a very different speed profile.

2. Does Mercedes protect the championship or attack the race?

With Antonelli leading and two Mercedes drivers tied behind him, strategic priorities can become complicated. A team maximizing constructor points may make different choices from a driver chasing a title.

3. Who owns the braking zones?

Monza's defining moments often begin under braking: the first chicane, Roggia and the approach to Ascari. Confidence on entry can create overtaking opportunities, but lockups and first-lap congestion can erase a weekend instantly.

Weekend schedule

Practice 1 and Practice 2 run Friday, September 4. Practice 3 and qualifying follow Saturday, September 5, with the Grand Prix on Sunday, September 6.
