Las Vegas GP FP3: Russell Leads Mercedes 1–2 Before Qualifying

George Russell topped the first dry practice session of the Las Vegas weekend with a 1:34.041 in FP3, leading Mercedes team-mate Kimi Antonelli by 0.911 seconds. Lando Norris was third for McLaren, 0.974 seconds off Russell’s pace. Earlier, Thursday’s wet FP2 had also been led by Russell, while FP1 produced no official timed laps.

FP3: Russell leads Mercedes one-two on the first dry track

Friday’s final practice provided the first dry running of the weekend after Thursday’s rain. Russell set the benchmark at 1:34.041 over 30 laps. Antonelli made it a Mercedes one-two on 1:34.952, with Norris third on 1:35.015. Charles Leclerc was fourth and Alpine’s Franco Colapinto fifth, just seven thousandths behind the Ferrari driver.

Max Verstappen finished sixth for Red Bull, 1.309 seconds slower than Russell. Lewis Hamilton was eighth, Pierre Gasly ninth and Arvid Lindblad tenth. Oscar Piastri ended the session 12th. These are practice positions, not the qualifying or starting-grid classification.

Qualifying outlook: Mercedes pace, but grid penalties matter

Russell’s advantage gives Mercedes the headline entering qualifying, but a practice gap does not guarantee pole. Norris, Leclerc and the Red Bull drivers still have qualifying runs to complete. Colapinto’s fifth place is an encouraging one-lap showing for Alpine, not a forecast of his grid position.

Russell and Verstappen made wheel-to-wheel contact near Turn 14 late in FP3. Reports also identified another incident involving Verstappen and Colapinto. Both were referred for stewards’ attention; no outcome is assumed here. Any subsequent stewards’ ruling and the confirmed qualifying classification will supersede this preview.

Antonelli’s five-place drop, the 10-place penalties for Piastri, Sainz and Stroll, and Bearman’s cumulative penalty mean the starting grid will not necessarily mirror qualifying positions. The FIA starting-grid document, once issued, is the authoritative source.

FP1 offered little running

Drivers who ventured out in the opening session mostly completed installation laps. Ryō Hirakawa and Rafael Câmara were among the first out for Haas, but the low grip left little opportunity to assess the car. FIA FP1 report

Late laps settled FP2

Teams began on full wets and moved to intermediates as grip improved. Lando Norris set the early pace on intermediates before Leclerc and Isack Hadjar took turns at the top. Debris near the final corner brought out a late red flag. After the restart, Russell and Gasly both improved to take the top two spots.

What to watch in qualifying

FP3 supplied the first dry reference, but qualifying at 8:00 p.m. PST will establish the one-lap order. Watch tyre preparation, clear-air management and whether Mercedes can repeat its dry practice advantage; the official grid will also reflect the previously announced power-unit penalties.

FIA grid penalties before qualifying

The stewards issued five power-unit decisions after Thursday practice. The starting order will depend on qualifying and how the penalties are applied.

  • Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes) drops five places.
  • Oscar Piastri (McLaren), Carlos Sainz (Williams) and Lance Stroll (Aston Martin) each drop 10 places.
  • Ollie Bearman (Haas) received a cumulative 35-place penalty and will be placed at the back of the grid if classified in qualifying.
