2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix Preview: Cold Tyres, High Speeds and the Final Championship Battles

Pirelli is bringing its three softest dry compounds to Las Vegas. Getting them up to temperature will matter on a circuit where long straights give the tyres time to cool before big braking zones.

Circuit intelligence

The long run down the Strip rewards straight-line speed, but the slow corners demand traction and confidence under braking. Turn 14 is a place to watch both qualities together. A car that brakes well at the end of the straight still needs to put its power down on exit.

Pulling wing angle out of the car can help on the straights, but it can also make the car harder to settle in slower corners. Look for which teams can carry speed through those corners while staying competitive on the Strip. Formula 1 event guide

How cold tyres could shape the weekend

Pirelli has nominated C3 as the hard, C4 as the medium and C5 as the soft. The supplier cited Las Vegas’s typically low temperatures and the need to bring tyres up to operating temperature quickly. On Friday, watch whether drivers can get the C5 ready by the start of a flying lap or benefit from an extra preparation lap.

For Saturday, the C4 offers a starting point for comparing long runs. The C3 could extend a stint if drivers can bring it into range quickly enough after a pit stop. Thursday’s evening practice gives teams a chance to compare warm-up and lap-time drop-off near the scheduled race start time. Pirelli’s compound announcement

Driver form and championship positions

Lando Norris arrives after winning from pole in São Paulo. Max Verstappen climbed from 21st to second and Isack Hadjar finished third, giving Red Bull a double podium. Interlagos showed what those drivers could do in changing conditions. Las Vegas will test whether they can find grip early in a lap and sustain it as the tyres cool on the straights. São Paulo post-race debrief

Kimi Antonelli has secured the Drivers’ Championship, and Mercedes clinched the Constructors’ Championship in Brazil. Verstappen sits second on 279 points, 17 clear of George Russell (262). Lewis Hamilton (261) is one point behind Russell, while Charles Leclerc (249) is three ahead of Norris (246).

Ferrari is second in the teams’ standings on 510 points. Red Bull (415) holds third by 17 points over McLaren (398). Official driver standings · Official team standings

Piastri faces a 10-place grid drop as McLaren brings upgrades

November 18 update. Oscar Piastri is set for a 10-place grid penalty after a reliability issue prompted McLaren to fit his fifth internal combustion engine of the season. The penalty applies to the starting grid. His exact starting position will depend on qualifying and how the FIA applies any other grid penalties.

McLaren will use practice to evaluate a new floor and changes intended to reduce drag before deciding which parts to race. Both cars are due to receive Mercedes’ updated turbocharger, which McLaren expects to deliver a small performance gain.

Red Bull leads McLaren 415–398 in the fight for third in the Constructors’ Championship. Watch whether Piastri can regain places under braking at the end of the Strip and how quickly he can bring his tyres up to temperature after a pit stop. McLaren’s weekend preview · Race Weekend Center

Haas confirms its Las Vegas lineup

Rafael Câmara will make his second Formula 1 start alongside Ollie Bearman in Las Vegas after making his debut at Interlagos. The weekend gives Câmara his first running on the Strip Circuit ahead of his full-time Haas seat in 2027.

Reserve driver Ryō Hirakawa will take Bearman’s car for FP1 at 4:30 p.m. PST on Thursday, November 19. It will be his fourth FP1 outing of the season with Haas. Hirakawa scored a point on his Formula 1 race debut in Mexico City, finishing tenth. Haas team announcement

Three things to watch

  1. Thursday’s second practice. Compare tyre warm-up and lap-time drop-off across each driver’s run in the 8 p.m. session.
  2. Friday’s qualifying preparation. Watch how much space drivers leave on their out-laps and when they start pushing to build temperature.
  3. Saturday’s first pit stops. Check whether drivers on fresh tyres gain time straight away or need a lap to get up to speed. That will help explain who can gain track position by stopping first.
