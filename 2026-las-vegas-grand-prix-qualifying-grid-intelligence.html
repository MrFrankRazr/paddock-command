Antonelli Fastest in Las Vegas Qualifying as Verstappen Inherits Pole

Kimi Antonelli delivered the fastest lap of Las Vegas Grand Prix qualifying, a 1:33.893, but his existing five-place power-unit penalty changes the race's front-row picture. Max Verstappen qualified second and is in line to inherit pole position, with George Russell alongside him, subject to the FIA starting-grid publication.

Antonelli beats Verstappen — but the grid tells another story

Antonelli carried his FP3 form into qualifying and improved to 1:33.893 in Q3. Verstappen's 1:34.151 was 0.258 seconds slower, while Russell qualified third on 1:34.224, only 0.073 seconds behind the Red Bull. Lando Norris took fourth at 1:34.244, just two hundredths behind Russell.

The raw qualifying result is not the starting grid. Antonelli had already received a five-place grid penalty for exceeding his internal combustion engine allocation. Applying that sanction promotes Verstappen to the front of the provisional race-order picture and Russell to the other front-row position; the FIA's published starting grid remains authoritative if further decisions alter the order.

Ferrari misses Q3 as mid-grid rivals advance

Both Ferraris were eliminated in Q2: Charles Leclerc qualified 11th on 1:35.197 and Lewis Hamilton 12th on 1:35.253. In contrast, Racing Bulls placed both Arvid Lindblad and Liam Lawson into Q3, while Alpine's Pierre Gasly and Franco Colapinto also reached the final segment. That gives the mid-grid fight a different shape to Friday's dry practice order.

Penalties and an unresolved back-row question

Antonelli faces five places, while Oscar Piastri, Carlos Sainz and Lance Stroll carry 10-place sanctions for power-unit component allocation breaches. Haas driver Oliver Bearman also has a cumulative power-unit penalty. Bearman was not classified in qualifying after a 1:48.005 Q1 lap, outside the 107% time of 1:41.290; his participation in the race is therefore subject to the applicable stewards' permission and regulations.

Because several penalties interact, Paddock Command will not publish an invented full starting order. Readers should use the subsequent FIA provisional or final starting-grid document for exact row-by-row positions, including any permissions to start and later amendments.

What to watch on race night

Verstappen versus Russell: the likely front-row pairing puts Red Bull and Mercedes together on the short run to Turn 1. Both demonstrated strong one-lap performance in qualifying, but long-run pace and tyre behaviour remain the decisive unknowns.

Antonelli's recovery: the fastest qualifier must work through traffic instead of starting at the front. Las Vegas rewards straight-line speed and decisive braking, but colder tyres can complicate overtaking attempts after starts and restarts.

Ferrari's recovery: Leclerc and Hamilton start with an on-track qualifying deficit to the Q3 runners; strategy and the final post-penalty grid positions will determine the opportunities available.

Race: Saturday, November 21, 8:00 p.m. PST · 50 laps around the 6.201 km Las Vegas Strip Circuit. This report covers provisional qualifying; the race grid and any new FIA decisions can supersede details above.
