Mexico City GP Preview: Antonelli’s Title Match Point, Thin-Air Test and Pérez’s Homecoming

Kimi Antonelli arrives at the 2026 Mexico City Grand Prix 123 points clear of George Russell. Ninth place is enough to clinch his first Formula 1 world title, but thin air, soft-compound strategy and a resurgent chasing pack make this more than a coronation lap.

Friday FP1 is also expected to feature ten temporary driver substitutions, including Ayumu Iwasa for Max Verstappen and Ryō Hirakawa for Esteban Ocon and Nikola Tsolov for Liam Lawson. The championship equation: why ninth place is enough

After winning the United States Grand Prix on October 25, Antonelli has 365 points, Russell 242 and Lewis Hamilton 241. The Mercedes driver leads Russell by 123 and Hamilton by 124 with five Grands Prix remaining, including Mexico City. Four races will remain after Sunday, with a maximum of 100 Grand Prix points available to any driver.

A ninth-place finish scores two points. Even if Russell wins Mexico City and collects 25, Antonelli would leave with a 100-point advantage. That is sufficient on countback: Antonelli already has nine victories in 2026, while Russell has three; even winning all five remaining races would leave Russell with only eight wins. The same P9 result also covers Hamilton and every other challenger.

Tenth place would also be enough unless Russell wins. Antonelli can even clinch without scoring if neither Russell nor Hamilton wins the race. Those are mathematical possibilities, not a forecast of the finishing order. No championship trophy has yet been secured.

Why Mexico City is a different technical test

The 4.304-kilometre Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez sits more than 2,000 metres above sea level and stages a 71-lap Grand Prix. Thin air reduces aerodynamic load and makes cooling management a bigger concern, even when teams use high-downforce wing settings. A car that worked well through Austin’s flowing corners may not carry the same advantage into Mexico.

The long run to Turn 1 also creates a distinctive opening-lap problem. Formula 1 places the first major braking point roughly 830 metres beyond pole position, giving the cars behind an opportunity to use the slipstream before the field compresses into the opening complex. Later in the lap, the stadium section rewards precision at relatively low speed.

The 2025 race offers a useful reference, not a prediction: Lando Norris won from pole ahead of Charles Leclerc and Max Verstappen. Verstappen has five Mexico wins, the most of any driver. Current form and 2026 car characteristics will matter more than past results alone.

Tyres: Pirelli goes softer than Austin

Pirelli has nominated C3 as Hard, C4 as Medium and C5 as Soft for Mexico City. That is the softest trio in its 2026 range and a step softer at the hard end than last year’s Mexico selection. Austin used C2/C3/C4, so direct conclusions from the Texas one-stop strategies would be premature.

The strategic questions are whether the C3 can deliver a long, competitive stint; how quickly the C5 degrades in race conditions; and whether the warm-up advantage of the softer tyres is worth an extra stop. Friday’s long runs and Saturday’s track evolution should provide the first meaningful answers. No race-winning strategy can be established before those sessions.

Driver form: Antonelli leads, but Ferrari and Red Bull are closing the argument

Antonelli’s Austin pole-to-win performance was his ninth victory of the season, achieved with a controlled one-stop medium-to-hard strategy. Russell finished eighth in Texas, leaving the title gap at 123 points. Antonelli has the strongest championship position; Russell’s remaining opportunity depends on an extraordinary swing.

Ferrari enters with Charles Leclerc on consecutive second-place finishes and Lewis Hamilton fourth in Austin. Verstappen took third in Texas, extending the six-race podium streak tracked by Paddock Command, and brings five previous Mexico victories. Norris finished fifth in Austin after tyre difficulties; McLaren needs stronger race execution if it is to resist Red Bull in the Constructors’ Championship.

The team contest is less settled than the Drivers’ Championship. Mercedes leads with 607 points, Ferrari has 474, and Red Bull and McLaren are tied on 355. That tie makes every point in Mexico consequential even if Antonelli seals the individual title.

Pérez signs Cadillac extension through at least 2029 before milestone home race

Cadillac confirmed on Wednesday, October 28, that Sergio Pérez has signed a multi-year extension keeping him with its Formula 1 team until at least 2029. The agreement adds long-term certainty to the American entrant’s first season and arrives just before Pérez is due to make his 300th Formula 1 start at his home Grand Prix. The six-time Grand Prix winner returned to the grid with Cadillac in 2026 after a year away from racing.

The commitment is significant because Cadillac is still building its competitive foundations. Neither Pérez nor teammate Valtteri Bottas has scored a championship point for the new team this season, and the 2027 second race seat has not been definitively settled. Pérez’s extension confirms his long-term future; it does not by itself establish Bottas’s 2027 status or Colton Herta’s eligibility for a race seat.

Cadillac leadership has framed Pérez’s experience and technical feedback as a source of continuity while the team develops. In Mexico, the immediate sporting task remains difficult: the altitude raises cooling demands, tyre preparation matters, and a packed home crowd cannot manufacture lap time. The Mexico City organisers scheduled a Pérez/Cadillac public show run on Paseo de la Reforma for October 28 as part of the 300-start celebrations; this article does not assume an unverified demonstration result.

Weekend weather: small Friday rain risk, hot qualifying, dry race forecast

Formula 1’s October 28 forecast gives Friday practice a 30% chance of rain with a high around 25°C. Saturday’s forecast reaches about 27°C with a 20% rain chance and potentially hot track temperatures. Sunday currently carries a 0% rain forecast and a high near 27°C. These are forecasts, not observed conditions, and may change before each session.

The possible Friday showers matter more than the percentages alone: with ten regular drivers expected to miss FP1, disrupted running would leave less representative dry-track data for their FP2 preparations. Tyre degradation, cooling and qualifying simulations still need on-track validation.

Mexico City weekend timetable

The 2026 Mexico City Grand Prix is Round 19, with practice on Friday, October 30, qualifying on Saturday, October 31, and the 71-lap race on Sunday, November 1. The event is not scheduled as a Sprint weekend.

Friday: FP1 12:30 p.m. Mexico City / 11:30 a.m. Pacific Daylight Time; FP2 4:00 p.m. Mexico City / 3:00 p.m. PDT. Saturday: FP3 11:30 a.m. Mexico City / 10:30 a.m. PDT; qualifying 3:00 p.m. Mexico City / 2:00 p.m. PDT. Sunday: race 2:00 p.m. Mexico City / 12:00 p.m. Pacific Standard Time.

Pacific clocks return to standard time early on Sunday, November 1, which is why the Sunday race conversion differs from the Friday and Saturday sessions. All session times are scheduled and subject to change.

Friday FP1 watch: ten practice-driver substitutions

Mexico City’s opening practice session now has a second major storyline alongside Antonelli’s title match point: ten teams are scheduled to field temporary driver substitutions for Friday, October 30. The updated list reflects October 28 team announcements and reporting, including Racing Bulls’ confirmation of Tsolov; final participation remains subject to the official FIA entry list. These are FP1 assignments, not changes to the Grand Prix race lineup.

  • Mercedes: Frederik Vesti is scheduled to replace George Russell.
  • Red Bull: Ayumu Iwasa is reported to replace Max Verstappen.
  • McLaren: Leonardo Fornaroli is scheduled to replace Lando Norris.
  • Cadillac: Colton Herta is scheduled to replace Valtteri Bottas.
  • Alpine: Gabriele Minì is scheduled to replace Franco Colapinto.
  • Racing Bulls: Nikola Tsolov is confirmed to replace Liam Lawson for his first official Formula 1 practice session.
  • Haas: Ryō Hirakawa is scheduled to replace Esteban Ocon.
  • Williams: Luke Browning is scheduled to replace Alex Albon.
  • Audi: Freddie Slater is scheduled to replace Gabriel Bortoleto.
  • Aston Martin: Jak Crawford is scheduled to replace Lance Stroll.

The competitive consequence is the reduced personal track time for several regular drivers. Russell, second in the championship, and front-running rivals Verstappen and Norris will rely on team FP1 data and their own FP2 running to refine the setup. With the circuit more than two kilometres above sea level, cooling checks and energy management make that lost hour especially relevant. Racing Bulls has confirmed Tsolov’s debut in Lawson’s car. Red Bull’s Iwasa assignment is supported by October 28 reporting; the final FIA entry list remains authoritative.

Herta’s additional Cadillac outing follows his Austin appearance and offers another chance to collect meaningful mileage. His eventual FIA Super Licence position and any 2027 race-seat implications should not be treated as settled by this assignment. The first on-track evidence arrives with FP1 at 12:30 p.m. Mexico City time (11:30 a.m. Pacific Daylight Time) on October 30.

What to watch when cars hit the track

The first practice benchmark is not simply the fastest lap. Watch the speed-trap readings against corner performance, brake and power-unit cooling, and the gap between qualifying simulations and long-run tyre degradation. Mexico’s altitude can reward a different compromise than Austin.

The second checkpoint is qualifying: the tow on the main straight and traffic on a short lap can affect track position before Sunday’s long launch toward Turn 1. The final checkpoint is whether Antonelli can collect the two points he needs without taking unnecessary risks, while Ferrari, Red Bull and McLaren fight for the podium and constructors’ points.
