MEXICO CITY GRAND PRIX · POST-RACE DEBRIEF · NOVEMBER 1, 2026

Verstappen Wins Mexico City; Antonelli Clinches Historic 2026 F1 Title

Max Verstappen won the 2026 Mexico City Grand Prix ahead of Lewis Hamilton and Kimi Antonelli. Antonelli’s P3 finish made the 20-year-old Mercedes driver Formula 1’s youngest-ever world champion. Hamilton finished 0.799s behind Verstappen after a race interrupted by a red flag following Valtteri Bottas’s crash. The race marked a major championship milestone with four Grands Prix remaining in the 2026 season.

Race debrief: Verstappen wins, Antonelli makes history

Max Verstappen delivered victory over 71 laps at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez, crossing the line 0.799 seconds ahead of Ferrari’s Lewis Hamilton. Mercedes driver Kimi Antonelli completed the podium, 4.006 seconds behind the winner, to secure the 2026 Drivers’ World Championship. At 20, Antonelli becomes Formula 1’s youngest-ever champion, surpassing Sebastian Vettel’s previous record. He is also the first Italian Drivers’ Champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953.

How the race unfolded

Hamilton arrived on pole following his first Ferrari pole position, but opening-lap pressure reshaped the lead fight. Charles Leclerc was given a five-second penalty after gaining an advantage by going off track. Verstappen took the lead during the pit-stop cycle as Ferrari served Leclerc’s penalty, then had to defend against Hamilton after a later interruption.

Valtteri Bottas crashed at Turn 1 following a suspension failure, prompting a safety car and red flag for barrier repairs. The restart brought Hamilton into second place. Antonelli overtook Leclerc for third, placing the championship beyond doubt while Verstappen resisted Hamilton’s pursuit. Another safety-car interruption followed contact involving Arvid Lindblad, but Verstappen held on at the final restart.

Championship watch: title clinched

Antonelli arrived with 365 points, 123 ahead of George Russell, and needed ninth place or better to guarantee the title. He finished third instead, confirming his maiden championship with four rounds remaining. The race result adds 15 points to Antonelli’s pre-race tally, taking him to 380 before any later official classification amendments. Russell finished sixth and Hamilton second.

Driver-form and race notes

Verstappen: Converted fifth on the grid into victory, his third win in four races. Hamilton: Turned Ferrari pole into second, finishing within one second of Verstappen. Antonelli: Rose from fourth on the grid to the podium and the championship. Leclerc: Finished fourth after a five-second penalty. Lawson: Recovered from a pit-lane start, following his qualifying disqualification and setup changes, to score ninth. Hirakawa: Finished 13th on his Haas Formula 1 race debut.

What comes next

The championship moves to the São Paulo Grand Prix on November 6–8. The Drivers’ title is decided; remaining rounds still offer race wins and championship-position battles. Monitor subsequent FIA stewards decisions for any classification changes.

Hamilton leads the published grid as Lawson falls to P22

Lewis Hamilton ended a pole-position drought dating to the 2023 Hungarian Grand Prix on Saturday, October 31, producing a final Q3 lap of 1:16.514 at the Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez. The seven-time champion beat former Mercedes team-mate George Russell by 0.009 seconds and secured the 105th pole position of his Formula 1 career — his first with Ferrari. Team-mate Charles Leclerc completed the top three, 0.119 seconds behind Hamilton.

Max Verstappen qualified fourth fastest with a 1:16.692, ahead of championship leader Kimi Antonelli on 1:16.707. However, the one-place penalty Verstappen received after Friday’s collision with Liam Lawson moves Antonelli into fourth on the published starting grid, with Verstappen fifth. Lando Norris starts sixth, followed by Isack Hadjar. Lawson originally qualified eighth, but the FIA subsequently disqualified his car from the qualifying results over a technical breach. Formula 1’s November 1 starting-grid page confirms Arvid Lindblad eighth, Pierre Gasly ninth and Nico Hülkenberg tenth. Lawson is listed 22nd and will race at the stewards’ discretion. Bottas is 21st following his power-unit penalty; Piastri advances to 12th and Hirakawa to 18th after the qualifying exclusion.

Q2 surprise: Piastri 13th as Alonso stops in Q1

McLaren’s Saturday practice promise did not carry through evenly into qualifying. Norris, who had led FP3 by 0.001 seconds over Antonelli, reached Q3 but could do no better than sixth. Oscar Piastri struggled for rear grip and exited in Q2 in 13th, behind Nico Hülkenberg (11th) and Franco Colapinto (12th). Piastri is listed P12 on the published starting grid following Lawson’s exclusion. Gabriel Bortoleto recovered from an FP3 power-unit issue to qualify 14th for Audi, ahead of Oliver Bearman and Carlos Sainz.

Fernando Alonso was told to stop his Aston Martin during Q1 with a suspected water-pressure problem linked to the energy-recovery system. The stoppage brought out a red flag and Alonso was classified 18th. Ryo Hirakawa qualified 19th for Haas in his first F1 qualifying session and is listed 18th on the published grid after replacing Esteban Ocon for the remainder of the weekend. Sergio Pérez and Valtteri Bottas were the final two qualifiers for Cadillac, with Bottas also carrying his previously reported cumulative power-unit penalty. The published Formula 1 starting grid lists Alonso 17th, Hirakawa 18th, Pérez 20th, Bottas 21st and Lawson 22nd. Late pre-race changes remain possible.

FP3 intelligence: Norris beats Antonelli by one thousandth

Saturday’s final practice had suggested a McLaren–Mercedes fight: Norris set 1:17.600, just 0.001 seconds quicker than Antonelli, while Leclerc was third, Piastri fourth and Verstappen fifth. Qualifying overturned that order as Hamilton, sixth in FP3, found the decisive lap and Norris dropped to sixth on the timing sheet. Practice is useful evidence of pace, but Hamilton’s pole illustrates how track evolution, tyre preparation and execution can reshape a close field.

FP3 top five: Norris 1:17.600; Antonelli +0.001s; Leclerc +0.117s; Piastri +0.195s; Verstappen +0.351s. Sergio Pérez and Gabriel Bortoleto recorded no timed FP3 lap amid separate power-unit work, but both later took part in qualifying. Official FP3 report ↗

Saturday driver change: Hirakawa replaces Ocon at Haas

Haas confirmed on October 31 that reserve driver Ryo Hirakawa would replace Esteban Ocon for FP3, qualifying and the Mexico City race, without disclosing a reason. Hirakawa had driven in Friday FP1, returned the car to Ocon for FP2 and finished 19th in Saturday FP3. He subsequently qualified 19th, ahead of his planned Formula 1 race debut on Sunday alongside Oliver Bearman.

The 32-year-old Japanese driver is a two-time World Endurance Championship title winner and a 2022 Le Mans 24 Hours winner. This is a full race-weekend substitution, not simply a Friday rookie practice appearance. No medical, contractual or disciplinary explanation for Ocon’s absence should be inferred. Official Haas change report ↗

Friday practice: Leclerc sets the benchmark twice

Leclerc led both Friday sessions: 1:18.128 in FP1 and 1:17.827 in FP2. Norris was 0.103 seconds behind in FP2, Russell 0.106, Antonelli 0.217 and Verstappen 0.311. Ferrari’s Friday pace proved relevant — but it was Hamilton, not Leclerc, who delivered the fastest qualifying lap on Saturday. The difference underlines the limitations of using practice classification alone to predict the pole sitter.

Friday also featured ten temporary FP1 driver substitutions. Hamilton’s FP2 was interrupted by a loss of power; his subsequent pole confirms he returned to competitive running for qualifying. Official FP2 report ↗

Antonelli returns and keeps the title equation intact

Antonelli completed both Friday sessions after missing Thursday media commitments while recovering from a viral illness. He finished second in FP1 and fourth in FP2. That confirms he returned to on-track running; it does not establish his medical condition beyond what Mercedes previously disclosed. His championship position is unchanged by practice: 365 points to Russell’s 242 and Hamilton’s 241.

Ninth place or better on Sunday still guarantees Antonelli the title even if Russell wins. He has nine Grand Prix victories this season, so a 100-point advantage after Mexico would be sufficient on countback with four races left. Russell’s FP2 third place, only 0.106 seconds off Leclerc, is a reminder that Mercedes has two cars in the fight for Saturday’s front rows.

FIA stewards: Lawson excluded after qualifying; Friday penalties remain

The stewards handed Verstappen and Lawson one-place grid penalties and one penalty point each following their FP2 contact. Verstappen qualified fourth fastest and is fifth on the published grid, promoting Antonelli to fourth. Lawson originally qualified eighth and was due to start ninth after his one-place penalty, but the separate post-qualifying MGU-K technical infringement supersedes that expected grid position; the published starting grid lists him 22nd. FIA Document 57 disqualifies Lawson from the qualifying classification; he is now listed 22nd, with permission to race at the stewards’ discretion.

Audi was fined €7,500 after FP1 substitute Freddie Slater left the garage with a cooling fan attached; that sanction is against the team, not a grid penalty for the driver. Cadillac’s Bottas faces a cumulative 50-place power-unit penalty and was the slowest qualifier. His final starting position must be confirmed from the official grid. Official stewards summary ↗

The championship equation: why ninth place is enough

After winning the United States Grand Prix on October 25, Antonelli has 365 points, Russell 242 and Lewis Hamilton 241. The Mercedes driver leads Russell by 123 and Hamilton by 124 with five Grands Prix remaining, including Mexico City. Four races will remain after Sunday, with a maximum of 100 Grand Prix points available to any driver.

A ninth-place finish scores two points. Even if Russell wins Mexico City and collects 25, Antonelli would leave with a 100-point advantage. That is sufficient on countback: Antonelli already has nine victories in 2026, while Russell has three; even winning all five remaining races would leave Russell with only eight wins. The same P9 result also covers Hamilton and every other challenger.

Tenth place would also be enough unless Russell wins. Antonelli can even clinch without scoring if neither Russell nor Hamilton wins the race. Those are mathematical possibilities, not a forecast of the finishing order. No championship trophy has yet been secured; Sunday’s race will determine whether Antonelli clinches in Mexico City.

Driver availability: both Antonelli and Bottas returned Friday

Mercedes’ October 29 announcement excused Antonelli from Thursday media duties while he recovered from a viral illness; he then ran in both Friday practice sessions, finishing P2 and P4. No change to his race entry has been announced. The first on-track evidence is encouraging for participation, but Friday lap times and Saturday qualifying cannot quantify the effects of illness.

Cadillac’s Bottas also missed Thursday media commitments and then took part in FP2, as planned, after Herta drove his car in FP1. The more significant new sporting development is Bottas’s cumulative power-unit grid penalty, detailed above. Pérez also drove in both Friday sessions, finishing 18th in FP2.

Why Mexico City is a different technical test

The 4.304-kilometre Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez sits more than 2,000 metres above sea level and stages a 71-lap Grand Prix. Thin air reduces aerodynamic load and makes cooling management a bigger concern, even when teams use high-downforce wing settings. A car that worked well through Austin’s flowing corners may not carry the same advantage into Mexico.

The long run to Turn 1 also creates a distinctive opening-lap problem. Formula 1 places the first major braking point roughly 830 metres beyond pole position, giving the cars behind an opportunity to use the slipstream before the field compresses into the opening complex. Later in the lap, the stadium section rewards precision at relatively low speed.

The 2025 race offers a useful reference, not a prediction: Lando Norris won from pole ahead of Charles Leclerc and Max Verstappen. Verstappen has five Mexico wins, the most of any driver. Current form and 2026 car characteristics will matter more than past results alone.

Tyres: Pirelli goes softer than Austin

Pirelli has nominated C3 as Hard, C4 as Medium and C5 as Soft for Mexico City. That is the softest trio in its 2026 range and a step softer at the hard end than last year’s Mexico selection. Austin used C2/C3/C4, so direct conclusions from the Texas one-stop strategies would be premature.

The strategic questions are whether the C3 can deliver a long, competitive stint; how quickly the C5 degrades in race conditions; and whether the warm-up advantage of the softer tyres is worth an extra stop. Friday supplied initial track evidence, but comparable long-run data and race-day track evolution remain essential. No race-winning strategy can be established from the practice and qualifying classifications alone.

Driver form: Hamilton and Ferrari lead the Sunday contenders

Hamilton’s first Ferrari pole and 105th career pole is the weekend’s decisive change in form. Leclerc, fastest in both Friday sessions, starts third and gives Ferrari two cars in the top three. Russell’s front-row start, just 0.009 seconds off Hamilton, makes him the closest immediate challenger to the polesitter.

Antonelli enters with nine 2026 Grand Prix wins and a 123-point lead over Russell, but qualified fifth fastest. Verstappen’s penalty moves Antonelli up to fourth on the published grid, a useful starting point for the two points needed to guarantee the championship. Verstappen qualified fourth fastest but drops to fifth; Norris is sixth, while Piastri, originally 13th in qualifying, is listed P12 on the published starting grid following Lawson’s exclusion. McLaren’s uneven qualifying result is relevant to its tight Constructors’ Championship contest with Red Bull.

Mercedes leads the Constructors’ standings with 607 points, Ferrari has 474, and Red Bull and McLaren were tied on 355 entering the weekend. No championship points have been awarded in practice or qualifying. Those totals remain pre-race.

Pérez signs Cadillac extension through at least 2029 before milestone home race

Cadillac confirmed on Wednesday, October 28, that Sergio Pérez has signed a multi-year extension keeping him with its Formula 1 team until at least 2029. The agreement adds long-term certainty to the American entrant’s first season and arrives just before Pérez is due to make his 300th Formula 1 start at his home Grand Prix. The six-time Grand Prix winner returned to the grid with Cadillac in 2026 after a year away from racing.

The commitment is significant because Cadillac is still building its competitive foundations. Neither Pérez nor teammate Valtteri Bottas has scored a championship point for the new team this season, and the 2027 second race seat has not been definitively settled. Pérez’s extension confirms his long-term future; it does not by itself establish Bottas’s 2027 status or Colton Herta’s eligibility for a race seat.

Cadillac leadership has framed Pérez’s experience and technical feedback as a source of continuity while the team develops. In Mexico, the immediate sporting task remains difficult: the altitude raises cooling demands, tyre preparation matters, and a packed home crowd cannot manufacture lap time. The Mexico City organisers scheduled a Pérez/Cadillac public show run on Paseo de la Reforma for October 28 as part of the 300-start celebrations; this article does not assume an unverified demonstration result.

Sunday weather and track conditions

Formula 1’s pre-weekend forecast, published October 28, projected dry conditions around 27°C for Sunday. That forecast is not a live race-day observation; teams and readers should check an updated forecast before the start. At Mexico City’s altitude, cooling capacity, brake temperatures and tyre warm-up remain significant variables even if rain stays away.

Friday FP1: ten temporary substitutions completed

Ten teams fielded young or reserve drivers in Friday’s opening practice, leaving Ferrari as the only team to run its regular pairing throughout FP1. These began as one-session assignments; Haas subsequently confirmed that Hirakawa would also replace Ocon for the rest of the weekend. The Friday substitutes were Frederik Vesti (Mercedes, for Russell), Ayumu Iwasa (Red Bull, for Verstappen), Leonardo Fornaroli (McLaren, for Norris), Nikola Tsolov (Racing Bulls, for Lawson), Ryō Hirakawa (Haas, for Ocon), Colton Herta (Cadillac, for Bottas), Gabriele Minì (Alpine, for Colapinto), Luke Browning (Williams, for Albon), Freddie Slater (Audi, for Bortoleto) and Jak Crawford (Aston Martin, for Stroll).

Vesti was third fastest, Iwasa fifth and Tsolov 12th. Slater’s Audi outing was interrupted after the car left the garage with a cooling fan still attached to the right radiator intake. The FIA stewards’ October 30 Document 22 confirms a €7,500 fine for Audi for releasing the car in an unsafe condition; the sanction was against the team, not a grid penalty for Slater. Crawford stopped on track late in FP1, prompting a Virtual Safety Car. Herta completed his Cadillac running and Bottas returned for FP2. The completed session confirms the rookie programme, but Saturday brought two separate developments: Haas promoted Hirakawa into the race seat, and specialist reports questioned Herta’s Super Licence points total.

Herta’s Super Licence status: one point still missing, reports say

Cadillac reserve driver Colton Herta completed Friday FP1 in place of Valtteri Bottas, but specialist reporting published on October 30–31 indicates the American remains on 39 of the 40 points needed for an FIA Formula 1 Super Licence. The Race reported that an error in the displayed 2025 IndyCar standings had placed Herta ahead of Felix Rosenqvist despite losing the tie-break; the corrected seventh-place classification reduced the Super Licence points from that season. Crash.net independently reported the same 39-point total.

Herta’s Mexico appearance was his fifth FP1 outing of the year, but the reports say only four of those outings earned qualifying Super Licence points. A further eligible FP1 appearance could supply the missing point, subject to FIA rules and confirmation. This is a reported eligibility clarification, not an FIA-issued race-entry decision; Cadillac has not announced a change to its Mexico City race drivers. The Race’s points breakdown ↗ · Independent report ↗. For previous context, see Herta’s Austin FP1 appearance.

Sunday race watch: title match point and Turn 1

Saturday’s answer was Hamilton: Ferrari converted its Friday pace into pole, but through its second car, with Russell just 0.009 seconds behind. Sunday’s first major test is the long slipstream run from the grid to Turn 1, where Russell, Leclerc and Antonelli will be positioned to challenge. The run to the first braking zone is roughly 830 metres, so pole is an advantage rather than a guarantee of leading the opening lap.

Antonelli’s priority is the title: ninth or better guarantees it, irrespective of Russell’s result. Russell must win to have any chance of extending the championship contest if Antonelli scores no points; Hamilton’s pole adds a separate Ferrari victory opportunity and a potential threat to the remaining mathematical contenders. Verstappen starts fifth after his one-place penalty. Lawson’s qualifying disqualification supersedes the earlier expected P9 position; Formula 1’s published grid now lists him P22.

Piastri qualified 13th but starts 12th after Lawson’s exclusion. Hirakawa is listed 18th for his expected F1 race debut; Cadillac’s Bottas is 21st and Lawson 22nd. The long run to Turn 1 and the possibility of early position changes remain central to Sunday’s race outlook.
