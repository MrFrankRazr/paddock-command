How the 27-lap tyre limit shapes strategy

Pirelli has selected its hardest dry-tyre trio: C1 hard, C2 medium and C3 soft. Lusail’s fast corners put sustained loads through the front tyres, which is why the supplier has opted for the harder end of its range. The main straight, more than a kilometre long, gives drivers their clearest chance to pass at Turn 1.

The 27-lap allowance is cumulative across the weekend, including Safety Car and Virtual Safety Car laps. A set used in practice or qualifying therefore starts the race with fewer laps available. Two new sets could cover only 54 of the scheduled 57 laps, so teams need to budget for at least three sets at full distance. An early Safety Car may offer a cheaper stop, but making that stop too soon can leave more laps than the remaining sets can cover.

Nine points separate Red Bull and McLaren

Kimi Antonelli (402 points) and Mercedes (666) have secured the titles. Ferrari sits second in the Constructors’ Championship on 517. McLaren scored 33 points in Las Vegas to Red Bull’s 25, cutting the gap for third from 17 to nine. Max Verstappen won the race, but Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri finished second and third while Isack Hadjar finished outside the points. Getting both cars into the points could decide which team heads to Abu Dhabi in third.

Verstappen holds second in the Drivers’ Championship on 304, 37 clear of Lewis Hamilton (267). George Russell and Norris are level on 264, three points behind Hamilton. Charles Leclerc is sixth on 250. With two Grands Prix remaining, Lusail offers Norris a chance to build on a win in São Paulo and second place in Las Vegas.

Pérez faces a five-place grid drop

The Las Vegas stewards gave Sergio Pérez a five-place grid penalty for his next race after the collision with Rafael Câmara. If Pérez takes part at Lusail, the penalty applies there. His starting position will depend on qualifying and the FIA’s final grid.

Three things to watch

  1. Friday’s long runs. FP2 starts an hour later than the Grand Prix. Compare how the medium and hard tyres hold up on long runs in that evening session, while allowing for differences in fuel load and track conditions.
  2. Saturday’s tyre choices. Watch whether teams use scrubbed softs in early qualifying runs and how many fresh mediums and hards they leave for Sunday.
  3. Sunday’s pit windows. At the first stops, check who can use clean air to gain time on fresh tyres and who risks rejoining in traffic. The remaining laps on each set will determine how early teams can make their second stops.
