São Paulo GP Practice: Antonelli Leads Norris and Verstappen into Qualifying

Kimi Antonelli ended Friday at Interlagos with the fastest lap of the weekend so far, turning an 0.082-second deficit to Lando Norris in FP1 into an 0.253-second advantage over the McLaren driver in FP2. Mercedes, McLaren and Red Bull are all in the qualifying fight, while a shifting weather outlook and a full day of rookie stories add uncertainty.

Antonelli reverses Norris’s FP1 advantage

Norris opened Friday fastest, putting in a 1:11.717 for McLaren during FP1. Antonelli was second at 1:11.799, only 0.082 seconds away, while Max Verstappen claimed third with 1:11.953. Lewis Hamilton and George Russell followed in fourth and fifth. The session included multiple rookie substitutions, so the initial classification did not feature every regular race driver.

In FP2, with all regular drivers back in their cars, Antonelli improved to 1:10.763 — the only lap below 71 seconds. Norris ran 1:11.016 and Verstappen 1:11.022, separated by just 0.006 seconds. Russell backed up Mercedes’ benchmark with fourth place, 0.273 seconds off, and Oscar Piastri placed fifth. The times suggest a competitive front group but do not establish race pace: teams vary their fuel loads, tyre preparation and run programmes.

Mercedes has pace — and a Constructors’ Championship target

Mercedes arrived at São Paulo 126 points ahead of Ferrari, 630–504, and can secure the Constructors’ Championship this weekend by outscoring Ferrari by at least three points. The Friday comparison favoured Mercedes on the timing screens: Antonelli led FP2 while Russell placed fourth. Ferrari’s Hamilton was sixth and Leclerc seventh.

That is not a prediction of Sunday’s finishing order. Qualifying position, race strategy, weather and reliability will determine whether Mercedes can convert the advantage into points. The title already secured by Antonelli is separate from the unresolved team championship.

McLaren and Red Bull remain within striking distance

Norris was the FP1 pacesetter and second in FP2, and Piastri was fifth on Friday afternoon. Verstappen placed third in both sessions. His FP2 margin to Norris was just six thousandths of a second. None of that guarantees a Sunday result, but it puts all three teams in the immediate qualifying conversation. The influence of McLaren’s latest updates and longer-run tyre wear cannot be established from the single-lap classification alone.

Home drivers: Bortoleto in the top ten, Câmara learning fast

Gabriel Bortoleto finished eighth in both Friday sessions for Audi, a consistent opening for the Brazilian. Haas newcomer Rafael Câmara was 18th in FP1 and improved to 16th in FP2 during his first Formula 1 Grand Prix weekend at home. His progress should be judged in the context of adapting to a current-generation car, new procedures and rapidly changing track conditions.

Audi’s Nico Hülkenberg did not record an FP2 time after his car was sidelined by technical trouble. Separately, a near-miss between Câmara and Leclerc during FP2 drew attention from the stewards; the timing table should not be treated as confirmation of a penalty or final grid sanction.

Weather and tyre management could change the picture

Friday’s later session took place under overcast skies and cooler temperatures, with intermittent light rain threatening the running. Interlagos can produce fast-changing grip levels, and Saturday may ask different questions of the C3 hard, C4 medium and C5 soft compounds. Teams used longer runs later in FP2 to collect tyre data; publicly available fastest-lap results alone are insufficient to calculate a reliable degradation ranking.

Three things to watch on Saturday

  1. Antonelli versus Norris: Can Friday’s fastest driver retain his advantage when the teams prepare for qualifying?
  2. Verstappen and the Mercedes second car: Verstappen was within 0.259 seconds of Antonelli and Russell within 0.273 seconds in FP2.
  3. Weather and session execution: A change in grip, tyre warm-up or traffic could reshape the order before the race.
