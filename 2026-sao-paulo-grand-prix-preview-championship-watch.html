São Paulo GP Preview: Verstappen’s Momentum, Antonelli’s Crown and the Constructors’ Fight

Formula 1 heads straight from Mexico City to Interlagos for the November 6–8 São Paulo Grand Prix. Kimi Antonelli has secured the 2026 Drivers’ Championship, but Max Verstappen’s late-season run, Lewis Hamilton’s Ferrari pace and an unresolved Constructors’ Championship give the 71-lap race plenty of competitive weight.

What Mexico City changed

Antonelli arrived in Mexico City needing ninth place or better to clinch the title and instead finished third behind Verstappen and Hamilton. At age 20, the Mercedes driver became Formula 1’s youngest World Champion. The result settles the biggest question of the season before the paddock reaches Brazil, but it does not settle the fight for the remaining championship positions.

Verstappen converted fifth on the Mexico City grid into victory, his third Grand Prix win in four races. Hamilton finished only 0.799 seconds behind after taking pole for Ferrari. That combination makes the Red Bull–Ferrari battle the clearest short-term form story heading to Interlagos: Verstappen is second in the Drivers’ standings with 261 points, just two ahead of Hamilton on 259.

Mercedes arrives at Interlagos on 630 points, 126 ahead of Ferrari on 504. With four Grands Prix remaining, the Silver Arrows have their first chance to clinch the 2026 Constructors’ Championship. A maximum of 129 team points will remain after São Paulo, so Mercedes will secure the title if it outscores Ferrari by at least three points in Brazil. A gain of exactly three would leave a possible final tie on points, which Mercedes would win on its superior victory count.

A Mercedes one-two, first-and-third, or first-and-fourth finish would guarantee the championship regardless of Ferrari’s results. Ferrari must prevent Mercedes from increasing its advantage by three or more points to extend the contest to Las Vegas. Hamilton’s second place and Leclerc’s fourth in Mexico City show Ferrari’s potential, but Mercedes has the commanding lead. Behind them, Red Bull holds third on 382, 17 ahead of McLaren on 365.

Driver-form intelligence: five storylines to follow

Max Verstappen — momentum: Three victories across the past four Grands Prix have brought Red Bull back into the fight for individual race wins. Interlagos tests whether that form survives a different layout and tyre allocation.

Lewis Hamilton — qualifying and race execution: The Mexico City polesitter finished less than a second from victory. Ferrari has an opportunity to convert front-row pace into a stronger two-car result in the constructors’ fight.

Kimi Antonelli — racing without title pressure: The champion no longer needs to manage a mathematical title-clinching scenario. His Mexico City podium nevertheless shows that Mercedes remains a contender at the front.

George Russell and Charles Leclerc — points swing: Russell sits fourth on 250 points and Leclerc fifth on 245. Their five-point separation is a more immediate championship contest than the already-settled drivers’ title.

Gabriel Bortoleto — home-race focus: The Brazilian Audi driver returns to Interlagos after a points finish in Mexico City. His home crowd and the circuit’s changing conditions add a distinct storyline outside the podium battle.

Circuit intelligence: why Interlagos rewards adaptability

Autódromo José Carlos Pace is a 4.309-kilometre, anti-clockwise circuit with a scheduled race distance of 305.879 kilometres over 71 laps. The Senna S immediately challenges braking and car placement; the infield changes direction repeatedly before the long uphill acceleration back to the start-finish straight. Short laps can compress qualifying gaps, making clean traffic management and a strong final sector particularly important.

The elevation changes, corner-speed variety and exposed sections also make setup a compromise. Teams must balance straight-line efficiency with traction and stability through the slower middle of the lap. Any rain would increase the premium on tyre decisions and pit-wall timing, but no specific race-day forecast is treated as confirmed in this early preview.

Pirelli tyre watch: a softer choice than 2025

Pirelli has selected C3 as the hard, C4 as the medium and C5 as the soft for São Paulo. This is one step softer than its 2025 allocation. Pirelli says the choice may widen the viable strategy range, potentially bringing all three dry compounds into play. That is a scenario to test in Friday long runs, not a prediction of the winning strategy.

Friday practice should establish how the C5 performs over a qualifying lap and how quickly the C3 and C4 lose performance over longer stints. If track temperatures change sharply or rain interrupts sessions, those early comparisons may become less reliable.

Three things to watch before qualifying

  1. Can Verstappen keep the streak alive? Mexico City demonstrated Red Bull’s race execution; Interlagos will test whether it has the pace to fight Ferrari and Mercedes across different corner types.
  2. Can Mercedes clinch the Constructors’ Championship? Antonelli and Russell need to outscore Ferrari by at least three points to secure the team title at Interlagos. Hamilton and Leclerc must produce another strong two-car result to prolong the fight.
  3. Will the softest three compounds alter the pit-stop picture? Pirelli’s C3/C4/C5 selection and Interlagos’ variable conditions create genuine uncertainty until practice establishes degradation.
