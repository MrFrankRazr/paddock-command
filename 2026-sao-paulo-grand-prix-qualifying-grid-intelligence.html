Norris Takes São Paulo Pole as Grid Penalties Reshape Sunday’s Race

Lando Norris took pole at a rain-soaked Interlagos with a 1:27.470, ahead of George Russell and Kimi Antonelli in qualifying. But FIA power-unit penalties have rewritten Sunday's provisional starting order: Antonelli joins Norris on the front row, with Liam Lawson third. The race is scheduled for Sunday, November 8, at 14:00 São Paulo time (09:00 Pacific).

Rain turns qualifying into a survival test

Qualifying at the Autódromo José Carlos Pace was delayed by heavy rain and disrupted by two red flags. Visibility, standing water and rapidly changing grip made a competitive lap unusually difficult. Norris ultimately produced a 1:27.470 to take pole by 0.204 seconds over Russell, with newly crowned World Champion Antonelli 0.625 seconds behind the McLaren.

Charles Leclerc was fourth on the qualifying timing sheet and Liam Lawson fifth. Nico Hülkenberg, Isack Hadjar, Oliver Bearman, Fernando Alonso and Alexander Albon completed the Q3 top ten. Those qualifying positions must not be confused with Sunday's penalty-adjusted grid.

FIA provisional starting grid after penalties

The stewards' provisional grid, issued November 7 as Document 51, promotes Antonelli to second, Lawson to third, Hülkenberg to fourth and Hadjar to fifth. Russell drops from second in qualifying to seventh on the grid; Leclerc falls from fourth to 14th. Norris retains pole. Power-unit penalties turn the race into a recovery contest

Russell has a five-place penalty after additional power-unit elements were used; Leclerc has ten places. Verstappen and Pérez each have 30-place grid penalties, and Hamilton has a 75-place grid penalty for additional changes. The FIA provisional grid places Hamilton 20th, Verstappen 21st and Pérez 22nd. These are grid-position penalties, not deductions from championship points.

With the grid compressed by those sanctions, Lawson and Hülkenberg find themselves in unusually strong positions. Hadjar and Bearman also stand to start high up the field. Russell, Leclerc and the penalized drivers will need to balance overtaking opportunities with the risks of traffic, variable weather and Safety Car interruptions.

Mercedes has a front-row opportunity and a team-title target

Antonelli has secured the 2026 Drivers' Championship on 380 points, but the Constructors' Championship remains open. Mercedes entered São Paulo with 630 points, 126 ahead of Ferrari's 504, and can clinch the team title by outscoring Ferrari by at least three points in Sunday's race. With Antonelli provisionally second and Russell seventh, Mercedes has a chance to turn its qualifying speed into the result it needs. Ferrari's provisional 14th and 20th starting positions make its task much harder, although no race points have yet been scored.

In the Drivers' Championship, Verstappen held second on 261 points before this weekend, only two points ahead of Hamilton, while Russell had 250 and Leclerc 245. That makes the recovery drives relevant to the fight for the remaining championship places, even though Antonelli has already clinched the overall crown.

Brazilian drivers face different Sunday tasks

Rafael Câmara's first F1 qualifying ended in Q2 after he lost control and struck the barriers, bringing out a red flag. He was classified 13th in qualifying and is placed 11th on the provisional grid after penalties are applied. For a debutant, completing a clean first Grand Prix will be at least as useful an indicator as position alone.

Fellow Brazilian Gabriel Bortoleto hit the barriers in Q1 and qualified 22nd. He is listed 19th on the provisional starting grid, facing a recovery race from the back half of the field. The home crowd has two Brazilian drivers on the grid, but their Sunday objectives differ sharply.

McLaren: a pole and a second-car recovery

Norris has the best possible starting position, but teammate Oscar Piastri qualified 12th and is provisionally placed 10th after penalties. McLaren therefore has one car aiming to control the race from the front and another working through traffic. Wet-weather unpredictability and pit-stop timing could change the opportunities for both.

Three things to watch on race day

  1. Norris versus Antonelli off the front row: The run to the Senna S could set the early order, but the two cars' pace across full race stints matters more than one qualifying lap.
  2. Recovery drives and midfield opportunity: Russell starts provisionally seventh, Leclerc 14th and three major names from the final three positions. Lawson, Hülkenberg, Hadjar and Bearman have unusual chances to score heavily.
  3. Interlagos conditions: Saturday's rain demonstrated the track's volatility. Tire preparation, track evolution, Safety Cars and any fresh rain could reshape strategy.
