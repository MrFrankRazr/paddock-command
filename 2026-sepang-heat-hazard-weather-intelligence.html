Sepang Heat Hazard: FIA Activates Cooling Measures

Formula 1's return to Sepang now has an official environmental warning attached to it. The FIA has declared a Heat Hazard for Sunday's Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia after the Official Weather Service forecast a Heat Index above the regulatory threshold.

The declaration was confirmed on September 30 under Article B1.5.10 of the 2026 Formula 1 Sporting Regulations. The rule allows a Heat Hazard when the Official Weather Service predicts a Heat Index above 31.0°C during the Sprint or Race, or at the Race Director's discretion.

What the Heat Hazard changes

Once declared, the Heat Hazard remains in force for the competition. Teams must fit the required Driver Cooling System components, and the system must be functional and available for use at the start of the affected race. A driver may elect not to wear the personal cooling equipment, but the regulations still impose the corresponding system and ballast provisions.

Sepang was already a severe environmental test

Heat and humidity are part of Sepang's identity. Formula 1's official race-week information describes the circuit as a demanding combination of fast, flowing sections and long straights, with high humidity and intense heat testing driver fitness. Sudden tropical showers can also transform the circuit quickly.

That combination matters operationally. Cooling requirements, brake and power-unit temperatures, tyre behaviour and driver workload all become part of the same race-week equation. A shower can then reset grip and track temperature with little warning.

Weather remains a strategic variable

Formula 1's official outlook calls for a warm and humid weekend with sunny spells, light winds and the chance of a rain shower. Sepang's tropical climate means short-notice thunderstorms remain possible, so Paddock Command will treat exact rain probabilities as fluid until the sessions are closer.

The key point is no longer whether heat will be relevant: the FIA has formally activated the Heat Hazard provisions. The remaining uncertainty is how the heat combines with any rain and how quickly teams can establish representative tyre and cooling data.

Friday becomes a high-value data day

FP1 starts at 12:30 local time on Friday, October 2, with FP2 at 16:00. FP3 follows at 12:30 Saturday before qualifying at 16:00. Sunday's 56-lap Grand Prix starts at 15:00 local time.

With Formula 1 racing at Sepang for the first time since 2017, every dry long run will matter. Teams need current-generation data on tyre degradation, cooling margins, balance through Sepang's high-speed sections and the effect of the circuit's abrasive surface. Any rain interruption increases the value of every clean lap.

Championship pressure arrives with the weather

George Russell's Azerbaijan victory reduced Kimi Antonelli's championship lead to 66 points. Sepang therefore combines an unfamiliar modern-era setup problem with a title fight that has tightened entering Round 16.

Paddock Command watch points

Watch cooling configuration, long-run tyre degradation, setup divergence between teammates, any wet-running opportunity and whether teams sacrifice aerodynamic efficiency for additional thermal margin. Friday's two practice sessions should provide the first meaningful evidence.
