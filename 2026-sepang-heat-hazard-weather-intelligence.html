Sepang Heat Hazard: Cooling Measures Activated as Rain Threatens F1 Return

Formula 1's return to Sepang has gained a new operational complication: extreme heat has triggered the FIA's Heat Hazard provisions, while tropical rain remains a live variable across the weekend.

The FIA has triggered Heat Hazard measures

The FIA has declared a Heat Hazard for the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia after its official weather service forecast a Heat Index above 31°C during Sunday's race. Under the 2026 regulations, teams must have the prescribed driver-cooling equipment available and the Heat Hazard mass provisions apply. The declaration turns Sepang's expected heat from a general concern into a formal race-weekend operating condition.

Friday could deliver the harshest track conditions

Formula 1's official forecast calls for a Friday high of 32°C and a heat index reaching 37°C. The projected track temperature could climb to 53.85°C, with a 50% chance of precipitation. Those numbers make FP1 and FP2 critical for tyre evaluation: teams need representative long-run data, but a shower could interrupt the programme or rapidly reset track conditions.

Qualifying remains weather-sensitive

Saturday is forecast to reach roughly 32°C with 86% humidity, a heat index just above 36°C and a 40% chance of rain. Track temperatures could again exceed 53°C. At a circuit Formula 1 has not raced at since 2017, reduced dry running would increase the value of simulator preparation and make setup decisions harder to validate.

Race day: heat first, rain still in play

Sunday's official outlook carries a maximum air temperature of 31°C, a heat index around 35°C and a 30% chance of rain. Track temperature could reach 46°C. Sepang's tropical climate can produce rapid changes, so the percentage should be treated as a forecast rather than a guarantee of either a wet or dry race.

The tyre equation gets harder

Pirelli's C2, C3 and C4 selection already made degradation a central question for Sepang. High track temperatures can intensify thermal management demands, while rain or a rapidly cooling surface can reverse the problem and make warm-up and crossover timing decisive. That makes Friday's long runs, compound behaviour and any wet-weather running particularly valuable.

Why this matters strategically

Sepang combines long straights, fast loaded corners and heavy braking with heat and humidity. Teams therefore have to balance cooling requirements against aerodynamic efficiency while building a setup that can survive changing grip levels. A wet-to-dry or dry-to-wet transition would further amplify the strategic uncertainty.

What Paddock Command is watching

Our Friday intelligence will focus on cooling configuration, tyre degradation, long-run pace, compound warm-up and any setup divergence produced by the forecast. If rain arrives during practice, crossover behaviour and wet-weather confidence become immediate qualifying and race indicators.
