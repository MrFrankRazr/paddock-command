Sepang Heat Hazard: Mercedes to Race Without Cooling Vests

Mercedes faces Sepang without cooling vests

Mercedes plans to send both drivers into Sunday’s race without cooling vests, despite the FIA declaring Heat Hazard conditions. George Russell said the team had prioritized other car-development work rather than bringing its driver-cooling system to the reliability standard it wanted for Sepang.

The declaration requires teams to meet the prescribed driver-cooling equipment provisions; it does not necessarily require drivers to wear the vest itself. That distinction matters as Mercedes prepares for a demanding race in heat and humidity.

Kimi Antonelli is set to start third following Isack Hadjar’s five-place grid penalty, while Russell starts seventh. Antonelli enters the Grand Prix with a 66-point championship lead over Russell. Maintaining physical performance deep into the race is an added variable in their championship battle.

The FIA has triggered Heat Hazard measures

The FIA declared a Heat Hazard after its official weather service forecast a Heat Index above 31°C during Sunday’s race. Under the 2026 regulations, teams must have the prescribed driver-cooling equipment available and the Heat Hazard mass provisions apply. The declaration makes Sepang’s heat a formal operating condition rather than a general concern.

How the weekend forecast developed

The original official forecast issued September 30 projected a Friday high of 32°C, a heat index reaching 37°C and track temperatures potentially reaching 53.85°C, with a 50% chance of precipitation. Saturday’s outlook projected roughly 32°C, 86% humidity, a heat index above 36°C and a 40% chance of rain. These were pre-session forecasts, not observed conditions or a current live weather report.

Race day: heat first, rain still in play

The September 30 official race-day outlook projected a maximum air temperature of 31°C, a heat index around 35°C and a 30% chance of rain, with track temperature potentially reaching 46°C. Conditions can change rapidly in Sepang’s tropical climate; check the latest track weather before the start rather than treating these earlier forecast figures as guaranteed race conditions.

The tyre equation gets harder

Pirelli’s C2, C3 and C4 selection makes degradation a central question for Sepang. High track temperatures can intensify thermal-management demands, while rain or a rapidly cooling surface can reverse the challenge and make tyre warm-up and crossover timing decisive. Friday’s completed practice sessions provide the baseline for race-strategy analysis.

Why this matters strategically

Sepang combines long straights, fast loaded corners and heavy braking with heat and humidity. Teams must balance cooling requirements against aerodynamic efficiency while managing changing grip. For Mercedes, the absence of cooling vests makes driver condition another variable alongside tyre degradation, pit-stop timing and race pace.

What Paddock Command is watching

Watch driver condition and consistency late in the 56-lap race, particularly at Mercedes. Tyre degradation, the timing of any weather change and the gap between one-stop and two-stop strategies remain the other decisive race-day questions. Avoid drawing conclusions about individual driver fitness or performance before the race provides evidence.
