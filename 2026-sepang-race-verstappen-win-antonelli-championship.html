Verstappen Wins Rain-Hit Sepang Race as Antonelli Extends Championship Lead

Max Verstappen delivered Red Bull's first victory of 2026 in a rain-disrupted Bahrain Grand Prix staged at Sepang. Kimi Antonelli finished second to extend his championship lead, while Lewis Hamilton recovered from an early tyre gamble to complete the podium.

Antonelli's title lead over Russell

Red Bull-Ford breaks through

Verstappen converted pole position into his first Grand Prix victory of 2026, a landmark result for Red Bull Ford Powertrains in its first season. After 55 racing laps at the Sepang International Circuit, he crossed the line 2.307 seconds ahead of Antonelli, with Hamilton third and Charles Leclerc fourth.

The result gave Red Bull its first win of the season while preserving Antonelli's position at the top of the championship. Verstappen's margin was narrow at the finish, but his response to the shifting conditions proved decisive.

Rain rewrites the opening strategy

Heavy rain delayed the start and forced teams into an immediate tyre decision on a circuit that was drying unevenly. Verstappen, Antonelli and Russell chose intermediate tyres. Hamilton and Leclerc were among the drivers who gambled on slicks, a decision that cost them ground while the track remained wet.

Antonelli and Russell initially passed Verstappen to run first and second. Verstappen regained second from Russell before an early Safety Car, triggered by Valtteri Bottas spinning into the gravel, opened a pit-stop window. As the circuit dried, the leaders changed from intermediates to slicks. Red Bull selected soft tyres for Verstappen and Hadjar while Mercedes chose mediums; Verstappen overtook Antonelli after the restart and established himself at the front.

Hamilton turns an early gamble into a podium

Hamilton's initial slick-tyre decision left him deep in the field, reaching 18th as the damp surface punished Ferrari's early call. Rather than allowing that setback to define his afternoon, he used the improving conditions and subsequent race interruptions to recover positions. He finished third, 4.919 seconds behind Verstappen, while team-mate Leclerc recovered to fourth.

Ferrari's third- and fourth-place finish salvaged strong points from a race in which its opening strategy initially looked costly. The recovery is an important marker of Hamilton's racecraft, but the early loss of track position illustrates how unforgiving Sepang's weather crossover proved to be.

Russell's retirement changes the championship

Russell was a podium contender before his Mercedes stopped late in the race, turning a potentially productive Sunday into a retirement. Antonelli's second-place finish therefore produced a significant swing in the drivers' championship: his advantage over Russell increased to 84 points.

For Antonelli, this was not a victory, but it was a championship-strengthening result. For Russell, the immediate challenge is to recover momentum after a costly non-finish rather than allowing the Sepang setback to compound at the next round.

FIA launches investigation into Sepang software failure

The extraordinary disruption before the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia was not caused by the weather alone. The FIA has committed to a full investigation into the software problem that affected multiple Formula 1 cars during the initial starting procedure at Sepang.

After heavy rain delayed proceedings, several drivers encountered abnormal power or deployment behaviour while circulating at low speed. Lewis Hamilton was forced to stop his Ferrari on track and restart it, while eventual race winner Max Verstappen was among the other drivers affected.

The problem became serious enough for Race Control to abandon the initial starting procedure and return the field to the pit lane. Revised software was subsequently distributed before Formula 1 attempted another start. The intervention allowed the Grand Prix to proceed, but the FIA is now examining why the failure occurred and why several cars were affected simultaneously.

The episode adds another technical dimension to an already unusual Sepang weekend. Formula 1's new-generation machinery places considerable responsibility on software and energy-management systems, and an issue capable of affecting several cars during the same starting procedure warrants scrutiny beyond the immediate race result.

For Paddock Command, the distinction is important: Sepang was not simply a rain-delayed race. It exposed an operational vulnerability significant enough for the governing body to order a full investigation.

Paddock Command verdict

Verstappen's breakthrough victory and Antonelli's expanding championship advantage remain the sporting consequences of Sepang.

The FIA investigation now becomes the technical consequence. Formula 1 recovered sufficiently to complete the Grand Prix, but the championship moves to Singapore only days later. Any FIA findings, software revisions or procedural measures resulting from Sepang should therefore remain on the intelligence watch as the paddock reaches Marina Bay.
