Sepang Weather Watch: Heat and Cooling Move to the Front

Official local forecasts agree on a hot Sepang weekend but differ on exactly when rain may arrive, making thermal management the firm baseline and tropical weather the variable to keep watching.

Paddock Command editorial · Published Sep 29, 2026
Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia · Oct 2–4

Heat is the reliable part of the forecast

Formula 1 returns to Sepang this weekend with one weather conclusion already clear: teams and drivers should expect significant heat and humidity.

The Malaysian Meteorological Department's current district forecast for Sepang calls for a high of 33°C on Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Friday is presently forecast dry, Saturday carries morning rain in some places before a dry afternoon, and the district outlook is dry for Sunday.

There is an important reason not to treat that as a guarantee of a dry race. MetMalaysia's town-level Sepang forecast currently shows rain on Sunday morning, while Formula 1's own race-week outlook describes warm and humid conditions with the chance of a shower. At a circuit known for fast-developing tropical weather, the timing of rain remains less certain than the heat.

Paddock Command weather baselinePlan around heat and humidity. Treat rain as a live variable rather than a settled wet-race forecast.

Cooling becomes a performance decision

Sepang's temperatures matter beyond driver comfort. Hot, humid conditions increase the cooling burden on the car, and opening additional cooling capacity can carry an aerodynamic penalty.

That trade-off will be particularly important on a 5.543-kilometre circuit combining long straights with sustained fast corners. Teams need enough thermal margin to protect the power unit and other systems without giving away unnecessary aerodynamic efficiency.

The weather therefore connects directly to Friday's engineering programme: cooling configuration, tyre temperatures, long-run degradation and energy management all need a clean baseline before teams can decide how aggressively to configure the cars for qualifying and the race.

Friday should provide the first clean reference

The current district forecast makes Friday the most straightforward day on paper, with no rain forecast across the morning, afternoon or night and temperatures ranging from 24°C to 33°C.

If those conditions hold, FP1 and FP2 become especially valuable. Formula 1 has not raced at Sepang since 2017, and the current generation of cars has no recent Grand Prix baseline at the venue.

Saturday and Sunday remain weather-watch sessions

Saturday currently carries the clearest rain signal in the district forecast, with rain possible in the morning before conditions turn dry later in the day. The town forecast is broadly consistent on Saturday.

Sunday is less settled across MetMalaysia's two local forecast products: the district outlook is dry, while the town forecast shows morning rain followed by a dry afternoon. That disagreement is precisely why Paddock Command will avoid assigning a definitive wet- or dry-race label this early.

Paddock Command Take

The actionable Sepang weather story is heat first, rain volatility second.

Teams can already plan for temperatures around the low-30s Celsius and the cooling demands that follow. What they cannot yet lock down is the timing of tropical rainfall.

That makes Friday practice the first major intelligence checkpoint. If the forecast evolves, the weather picture should be updated again before qualifying rather than carried forward from an early-week percentage.
