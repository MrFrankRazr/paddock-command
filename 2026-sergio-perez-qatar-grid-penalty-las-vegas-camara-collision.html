Sergio Pérez Handed Five-Place Qatar Grid Penalty After Las Vegas Crash

Cadillac driver Sergio Pérez will take a five-place grid penalty at the Qatar Grand Prix after stewards ruled him responsible for a collision with Haas newcomer Rafael Câmara during the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

What happened in Las Vegas?

Approaching Turn 14 during the race on November 21, Pérez attempted to pass Câmara. Contact between the Cadillac's front-right and the Haas's rear-left sent both cars into the barriers. Pérez retired, while Câmara continued and finished 16th. The collision prompted a Safety Car period.

Why the penalty carries over to Qatar

Following a post-race investigation, the stewards determined that Pérez had not established sufficient overlap to earn the corner. They assigned him responsibility and imposed a 10-second time penalty. Since he retired and could not serve it, that penalty was converted to a five-place grid drop at his next race, the Qatar Grand Prix.

What it means for Cadillac and the next race

The penalty gives Pérez an immediate recovery challenge at Lusail. Cadillac has yet to score its first championship points, and team-mate Valtteri Bottas finished 18th in Las Vegas. The exact position from which Pérez will start in Qatar cannot be determined until qualifying and any other grid adjustments are complete.

Las Vegas in context

Max Verstappen won the Grand Prix ahead of Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri, with Kimi Antonelli fourth. Pérez's penalty does not change that top-four finishing order. It is a separate Qatar starting-grid sanction, not a Las Vegas finishing-position adjustment.

Source: Formula 1, November 22 — stewards' post-race decision.
