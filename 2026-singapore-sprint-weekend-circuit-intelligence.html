SESSION INTELLIGENCE · OCTOBER 9, 2026

Verstappen Sets Sprint Qualifying Benchmark After Russell Leads Sole Practice

Max Verstappen topped Singapore Sprint Qualifying with 1:31.156, 0.120 seconds faster than George Russell, while Charles Leclerc finished third. McLaren's Oscar Piastri and Lando Norris placed fourth and fifth, Lewis Hamilton sixth and championship leader Kimi Antonelli seventh.

Classification note: Formula 1's initial report flagged a potential SQ1 yellow-flag infringement involving Verstappen. Check the final FIA stewards' decision before considering the pole position fully confirmed.

FP1: Russell Sets Benchmark in Mercedes Specification Test

Russell topped the only practice session with 1:32.274 on soft tyres, ahead of Leclerc by 0.198 seconds and Norris by 0.273. Hamilton and Piastri completed the top five. Russell used the previous Mercedes aerodynamic specification, while Antonelli continued with the new package and set his fastest time on mediums. The 1.251-second practice gap between the Mercedes cars does not demonstrate which specification is quicker because tyre compounds and programmes differed.

Verstappen Turns Medium-Tyre Pace into Sprint Qualifying Speed

Verstappen was sixth in practice but quickest of the medium-tyre runners. He then posted the quickest time in the SQ3 soft-tyre shootout. That progression builds on Red Bull's Sepang victory, but Saturday's tyre management and race pace remain unresolved questions.

Ferrari and McLaren Stay in the Fight

Leclerc placed third in Sprint Qualifying, with Hamilton sixth. McLaren locked out fourth and fifth, Piastri ahead of Norris. The result is encouraging after McLaren's recent straight-line speed concerns but does not itself prove that the team's reported technical correction has resolved the problem.

Championship Watch and Sprint Outlook

Antonelli holds 320 points, 84 clear of Russell's 236. Russell's back-of-grid penalty applies to Sunday's Grand Prix rather than the Sprint, leaving him a chance to collect Saturday points from the front row if the qualifying order is confirmed. The inaugural Singapore Sprint runs for 21 laps, followed later by qualifying for Sunday's Grand Prix.

Paddock Command verdict: Russell set the practice benchmark, but Verstappen found the decisive Sprint Qualifying lap. Ferrari and McLaren remain close enough to influence the Sprint; the split-spec Mercedes comparison needs representative race data before a technical winner can be declared.

Singapore Sprint Weekend: Championship, Circuit and Technical Context

Five days after Sepang, Formula 1 moves from a rain-hit high-speed race to the walls and heat of Marina Bay. Singapore's first F1 Sprint weekend gives teams only one practice session before competitive running begins.

FIA Confirms Wet-Weather Software Contingency for Singapore

Formula 1 enters its first Singapore Sprint weekend with an important technical safeguard following the disrupted start of last Sunday's Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia.

The FIA confirmed on October 8 that its investigation into the Sepang power-unit software malfunction has been completed.

The governing body's findings identified an interaction between low engine speeds and wet-weather power-reduction settings. During formation laps at Sepang, several cars experienced excessive power loss that could not be recovered within the affected circuit sectors.

The incident forced officials to suspend the start procedure while engineers developed and implemented revised software configurations.

The FIA has now confirmed that the configuration introduced at Sepang will be reinstated for the Singapore Grand Prix if similar weather conditions are expected. A further revision to the wet-weather software is scheduled for the United States Grand Prix on October 25.

Why this matters at Marina Bay

Singapore presents a very different challenge from Sepang. The 4.927-kilometre Marina Bay Street Circuit combines slow corners, frequent acceleration zones and barriers that leave little margin for error.

If rain affects the weekend, power-unit behaviour at low speeds could become an important operational consideration. The FIA's decision to retain the revised Sepang configuration gives teams a known technical response rather than requiring another emergency solution during the race weekend.

However, the configuration's availability does not guarantee that wet-weather running will be trouble-free. The FIA says it will continue strengthening software testing and verification alongside teams and power-unit manufacturers.

Heat hazard adds another technical requirement

The software announcement follows the FIA's decision to activate heat-hazard regulations for both Saturday's Sprint and Sunday's Grand Prix.

Forecast temperatures exceeding 31°C triggered the declaration. Teams must comply with the applicable driver-cooling requirements, while drivers retain the option of whether to wear the cooling vest.

The declaration adds another operational consideration to an already demanding Sprint weekend.

Haze remains under observation

Singapore continues to experience unhealthy air quality. The FIA and local authorities are monitoring conditions, with contingency arrangements available if the haze worsens.

Restrictions on outdoor entertainment remain possible, but officials do not currently expect racing to be disrupted.

Weekend schedule remains unchanged

Friday's FP1 and Sprint Qualifying have concluded. Saturday's Sprint begins at 17:00 Singapore time. Saturday's Sprint starts at 17:00, with Grand Prix qualifying scheduled for 21:00. Sunday's 62-lap Singapore Grand Prix begins at 20:00.

One practice session, then it counts

Marina Bay hosts its first Formula 1 Sprint weekend from October 9–11. The compressed format is the defining operational challenge: FP1 runs Friday from 16:30 to 17:30 local time, followed by Sprint Qualifying at 20:30. Saturday brings the 21-lap Sprint at 17:00 and Grand Prix qualifying at 21:00 before Sunday's 62-lap race at 20:00.

At a street circuit where confidence and track evolution matter, every installation lap and setup change in FP1 carries more weight than on a conventional weekend. Teams that miss the operating window early have little time to recover before the first competitive session.

Antonelli arrives with championship control

Kimi Antonelli leads the Drivers' Championship with 320 points, 84 ahead of Mercedes team-mate George Russell on 236. Lewis Hamilton is third on 214. Sepang widened the title gap after Antonelli finished second while Russell retired late, but Antonelli has cautioned that Mercedes still has work to do.

A Sprint weekend puts points on offer before Sunday's Grand Prix, so Singapore gives Russell an extra opportunity to respond — and gives Antonelli another chance to increase the pressure.

Can Red Bull make Sepang a trend?

Max Verstappen arrives after converting pole into Red Bull's first victory of 2026 at Sepang. Singapore is a very different test. Marina Bay emphasizes braking confidence, low-speed traction, mechanical grip and precise energy deployment rather than Sepang's long high-speed load profile. If Red Bull is competitive again, the Malaysia result will look less like a circuit-specific spike and more like meaningful late-season progress.

Marina Bay circuit intelligence

The current Marina Bay layout measures 4.927 km. Sunday's race is scheduled for 62 laps and 305.337 km. Formula 1 lists Lewis Hamilton's 1:33.808 from 2025 as the fastest race lap on the current configuration.

Singapore remains a high-concentration street event: barriers punish small errors, braking zones arrive quickly, and the night schedule combines high ambient stress with a surface that typically evolves as rubber builds through the weekend.

Ferrari and McLaren enter with different questions

Hamilton's recovery to third at Sepang gives Ferrari momentum heading to a venue where qualifying confidence can have an outsized effect on race position. Charles Leclerc remains fourth in the championship on 191 points.

McLaren, meanwhile, leaves Malaysia needing a cleaner conversion. Lando Norris and Max Verstappen are tied on 188 points in the standings, while Oscar Piastri sits seventh on 128. Singapore's Sprint format reduces the time available to solve balance problems if either car starts Friday outside its preferred window.

McLaren targets fuel-system problems before Singapore Sprint

McLaren arrives at Marina Bay attempting to recover performance following two difficult Formula 1 weekends.

The team has reportedly made changes to auxiliary power-unit components, including fuel-system-related parts, after investigating persistent straight-line speed problems affecting Lando Norris.

The difficulties emerged at Azerbaijan and continued during the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia. McLaren identified a fuel-injection-related problem contributing to Norris's straight-line deficit, although the team's overall performance difficulties cannot necessarily be attributed to a single cause.

The latest changes are intended to address the problem before Friday's opening practice session.

Norris wants confirmation on track

Norris acknowledged Thursday that McLaren has made progress investigating its recent difficulties. However, the team still needs to establish whether the corrective work delivers the expected improvement during competitive running.

Singapore offers a different technical challenge from Baku and Sepang. Marina Bay's slower corners, demanding braking zones and relatively limited emphasis on outright straight-line speed could help McLaren recover competitiveness.

Nevertheless, any remaining power-unit performance deficit could still prove costly during qualifying. With only one practice session before Sprint Qualifying, McLaren has limited time to validate its changes.

Constructors' Championship pressure

McLaren enters Singapore third in the Constructors' Championship with 316 points. Red Bull follows on 298, leaving 18 points between the teams.

Norris and Max Verstappen are also tied on 188 points in the Drivers' Championship, with Norris ahead on countback.

Verstappen arrives after winning at Sepang, while McLaren failed to secure a podium at either of the previous two rounds. A successful technical correction could help McLaren protect its Constructors' Championship position and restore Norris's competitiveness.

Paddock Command McLaren watch

Friday's opening practice session will provide the first competitive indication of whether McLaren's changes have improved Norris's straight-line performance.

The key questions are whether the previous deficit has diminished, whether McLaren has recovered qualifying competitiveness and whether Norris can challenge Verstappen and the Mercedes drivers during Sprint Qualifying.

Until representative track data is available, the effectiveness of McLaren's technical changes remains unconfirmed.
