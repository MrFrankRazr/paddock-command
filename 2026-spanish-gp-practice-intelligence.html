FRIDAY PRACTICE INTELLIGENCE · ROUND 14

Antonelli Sets the Madrid Benchmark as Norris Loses Crucial FP2

Kimi Antonelli ended Formula 1’s first Friday at Madring fastest, Ferrari remained immediately in contention and Lando Norris lost an entire FP2 session as teams began converting simulation into real Madrid data.

Formula 1 finally has a real reference point for Madring

After weeks of simulation, circuit projections and technical unknowns, Friday’s two practice sessions produced the first meaningful picture of how the new Madrid circuit is behaving in Formula 1 trim.

Mercedes emerged with the strongest headline. George Russell led FP1 with a 1:34.077 before championship leader Kimi Antonelli moved to the front in FP2 with a 1:33.662. The result continued Antonelli’s strong momentum after his victory from 19th at Monza and gave Mercedes the fastest driver in both Friday sessions.

Antonelli converts the first day into another positive signal

Antonelli’s FP2 benchmark was 0.113 seconds faster than Charles Leclerc and 0.149 ahead of Lewis Hamilton. Ferrari did not allow Mercedes to establish a clear performance gap, with both Leclerc and Hamilton inside 0.15 seconds of the championship leader. Russell finished fifth in FP2 after setting the pace earlier in the day.

Friday practice awards no points, but Antonelli has begun an unfamiliar weekend without showing an obvious adaptation deficit. He entered Madrid 66 points ahead of Russell after 13 rounds.

Ferrari is close enough to matter

Leclerc’s second place and Hamilton’s third in FP2 are significant because Madrid was expected to reward rapid setup convergence. Ferrari appears to have reached a competitive baseline quickly.

If Leclerc and Hamilton remain within this margin after FP3, Mercedes may face a genuine Ferrari qualifying threat rather than a straightforward intra-team fight.

The long runs shift the picture toward Mercedes and Verstappen

Friday’s single-lap times made Ferrari look immediately threatening. The longer runs tell a different story.

George Russell produced the strongest adjusted high-fuel pace, with Max Verstappen approximately 0.13 seconds per lap slower. Kimi Antonelli ranked third on the same comparison, around 0.55 seconds per lap behind Russell.

Ferrari and McLaren were less convincing in the race simulations. Ferrari remains a credible qualifying threat, but the early race-pace picture points more strongly toward Mercedes, with Verstappen appearing to be the closest challenger over a stint.

The new circuit requires caution because fuel loads, tyre choices and rapidly evolving grip can distort Friday comparisons. Even so, Mercedes leaves the first day with advantages in headline lap time and longer-run performance.

Tyre degradation may be higher than expected

Madrid’s tyre behaviour produced another surprise. Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff said degradation in FP1 reached roughly 0.8 seconds per lap, while later FP2 long runs were closer to 0.3 seconds per lap.

That leaves a two-stop race in play, although continued track evolution could reduce degradation before Sunday. The new asphalt is generating substantial grip while tyre warm-up remains difficult.

Pirelli’s interpretation was more conservative, suggesting degradation could ultimately be lower than teams initially feared. That disagreement makes Saturday’s running especially important.

Lindblad turns the interruption warning into reality

The first F1 red flag at Madring arrived in FP2 when Arvid Lindblad lost the rear of his Racing Bulls at Turn 13 and hit the barriers sideways. He was unhurt and still finished fourth fastest.

Friday does not prove that qualifying will become chaotic, but it strengthens the case for early representative laps on Saturday. A red flag at the wrong moment in Q1 or Q2 could carry significant consequences.

Norris has the biggest Friday recovery job

The most consequential lost mileage belonged to Lando Norris. McLaren changed the gearbox on Norris’s car and he did not participate in FP2.

At a familiar circuit, losing one practice session can sometimes be absorbed through accumulated historical data. Madring offers no such luxury. Every driver began this weekend without previous Formula 1 race experience here, making Friday mileage unusually valuable for setup correlation, tyre understanding and track learning.

Oscar Piastri finished seventh in the second McLaren, so FP3 becomes particularly important for the team.

Red Bull remains outside the immediate Friday fight

Max Verstappen finished sixth in FP2, while Liam Lawson was 10th as he continued substituting for the injured Isack Hadjar. It would be premature to rule Red Bull out of qualifying contention, but the team did not produce the early Madrid surprise needed to change the competitive narrative immediately.

Traffic is already part of the qualifying story

Friday also produced complaints about drivers obstructing one another. Around a circuit combining high-speed commitment, limited sight lines and large speed differentials between preparation and push laps, traffic management should join red-flag exposure as one of Saturday’s key execution variables.

What Friday actually tells us

Mercedes has the most complete Friday profile. Antonelli set the fastest single lap, Russell was fastest in FP1 and the longer-run evidence points toward Russell having the strongest race pace.

Ferrari remains dangerous over one lap, but its longer runs were less convincing. Verstappen may therefore represent Mercedes’ most credible current race-pace threat.

McLaren has the largest recovery job. Piastri’s race simulation did not match the leading pace and Norris lost almost the entire second session.

Saturday becomes a two-part test — whether anyone can displace Mercedes over a qualifying lap, and whether Friday’s apparent Mercedes race-pace advantage survives another round of setup changes.

Paddock Command Saturday watch list
Russell’s long-run pace: whether Mercedes retains its high-fuel advantage.
Antonelli: whether the FP2 leader can match Russell as setup work converges.
Verstappen: the strongest current non-Mercedes long-run threat, approximately 0.13s per lap from Russell.
Ferrari: whether its single-lap strength converts to longer-run pace.
McLaren: Norris’s recovery after losing FP2 and Piastri’s race pace.
Tyre degradation: whether Saturday confirms Friday’s high wear or track evolution reduces it.
Traffic and interruptions: major qualifying execution risks.

FP3 begins Saturday at 12:30 Madrid time, followed by qualifying at 16:00.

Madrid began as a simulation exercise. By Friday evening, it had become a real competitive problem — and Mercedes currently has the strongest answer.
