Norris Takes Madrid Pole as Antonelli Locks Down the Front Row

Lando Norris completed McLaren’s recovery from a difficult Friday by taking the first Formula 1 pole at Madring, edging championship leader Kimi Antonelli by just 0.011 seconds.

Paddock Command editorial · Published Sep 12, 2026
Qualifying complete · Next: Spanish Grand Prix

FRONT ROWAntonelli · +0.011

RACESun · 15:00 Madrid

Norris turns Friday disruption into the first Madring pole

Lando Norris will start the inaugural Formula 1 race at Madring from pole position after producing a 1:31.824 on his final Q3 run. The McLaren driver beat Kimi Antonelli by only 0.011 seconds, completing a remarkable recovery after losing almost all of FP2 to a gearbox change.

The result matters beyond the headline. Madrid has offered little margin for error all weekend, and overtaking remains an open question around a fast, narrow layout. Norris now controls the most valuable piece of track position available before Sunday’s first lap.

Antonelli protects the championship position

Antonelli missed pole by the smallest margin of the weekend but still secured the front row. He arrived in Madrid 66 points ahead of Mercedes team mate George Russell and has remained at the front throughout the event, topping FP2 and FP3 before qualifying second.

With Russell qualifying sixth, Antonelli begins Sunday in a position to extend his championship advantage without needing to force the race from the opening corner. The opportunity to attack Norris is real, but so is the value of another controlled points gain.

Verstappen keeps Red Bull inside the fight

Max Verstappen qualified third with a 1:31.964, 0.140 seconds away from Norris. Friday’s longer-run analysis had already placed Verstappen among the strongest race-pace challengers, and a second-row start gives Red Bull immediate strategic access to both front-row cars.

If that long-run strength carries into Sunday, Verstappen can pressure the leaders through tyre life and pit timing even if passing proves difficult on track.

Hamilton recovers from his FP3 crash

Lewis Hamilton qualified fourth in 1:32.013, only 0.189 seconds from pole, after Ferrari repaired the car he crashed at the final corner during FP3. Hamilton had briefly held provisional pole after the first Q3 runs before the final sequence dropped him to the second row.

Charles Leclerc qualified fifth, putting both Ferraris directly behind the leading trio and giving the team multiple strategic options for Sunday.

Russell faces a championship recovery race

George Russell qualified sixth, four places behind championship leader Antonelli. With the title gap already at 66 points, the grid positions create another opportunity for Antonelli to increase his advantage if Mercedes cannot move Russell forward.

Oscar Piastri starts seventh for McLaren, followed by Liam Lawson, Franco Colapinto and Arvid Lindblad in the remainder of the Q3 top 10.

Madrid’s interruption risk remains part of the race equation

The weekend has repeatedly punished mistakes. Hamilton and Ollie Bearman both crashed during FP3, while Bearman’s damage prevented Haas from getting him onto the track for qualifying. Lance Stroll also failed to set a qualifying time because of a water-pressure problem after Aston Martin had already fitted new power-unit components that trigger a grid penalty.

Those disruptions reinforce the strategic value of flexibility on Sunday. A Safety Car or red flag could rapidly alter the advantage created by track position and pit timing.

What qualifying changed

Friday suggested Mercedes had the most complete performance profile. Saturday showed that McLaren had enough speed to overturn that assumption over one lap.

Norris now owns pole, but Antonelli remains alongside him with the championship advantage and Verstappen starts close enough to turn the opening phase into a three-car strategic contest. Hamilton’s recovery keeps Ferrari within immediate range as well.

The Spanish Grand Prix starts Sunday at 15:00 Madrid time.

Madrid began as an unknown. By Saturday evening, the equation is clearer: Norris has pole, Antonelli has championship control, and Verstappen is positioned to make both of them work for it.
