F1 Will Shorten Grand Prix Distances in 2027 — Here’s What Changes

The F1 Commission has agreed to reduce the standard Grand Prix distance to 290 kilometres for 2027, alongside changes for interrupted races, practice sessions and gearbox development.

Paddock Command editorial · Published Sep 21, 2026
Commission approved · Subject to FIA World Motor Sport Council approval
2027 RACE DISTANCE290 km
REDUCTION15 km
TYPICAL EFFECT2–3 fewer laps
STATUSWMSC approval pending

Formula 1's 2027 season is taking shape as more than a calendar change.

The Formula 1 Commission has agreed a package of regulatory changes that would shorten Grand Prix distances, alter the rules governing disrupted races and give officials more flexibility when practice sessions are interrupted.

The most immediately visible change is simple: Formula 1 races are set to become shorter.

Grand Prix distance drops from 305 km to 290 km

Beginning in 2027, the standard Grand Prix distance would fall from 305 kilometres to 290 kilometres. The FIA says the 15-kilometre reduction will typically remove two or three laps, depending on the circuit.

The change accompanies refinements to Formula 1's 2027 power-unit regulations involving fuel flow.

Paddock Command watch pointA shorter distance slightly tightens the strategic window: less total fuel, fewer tyre kilometres and fewer laps to recover after a late stop or Safety Car intervention.

What the shorter distance could change

A reduction of roughly 15 kilometres is modest, but race distance influences calculations around fuel, tyres and pit windows. Two or three fewer laps reduce total tyre mileage and slightly alter the windows teams use when deciding when to stop.

Drivers attempting to extend a tyre stint will have less distance to survive, while cars chasing after a late pit stop will have fewer laps available to recover track position. The fundamental structure of a Grand Prix remains intact, but its strategic boundaries become slightly tighter.

Wet and disrupted races are changing too

For 2027, F1 plans to introduce a hard-stop time defining when racing activity must finish. At the same time, the existing three-hour elapsed-time limitation — which includes interruptions — would be removed.

The existing limit of two hours of actual racing remains. The stated objective is to give spectators the best possible chance of seeing a full race when disruptions occur while still establishing a definitive endpoint.

Practice sessions could be extended

The Commission also agreed to provide greater flexibility to extend Free Practice sessions when they are interrupted, potentially preserving more track time after lengthy red flags.

Gearbox development opens up

The Commission agreed to remove gearbox homologation for 2027 following consultation with the relevant manufacturer working group and a recommendation from the Sporting Advisory Committee. The FIA says this preserves supplier development freedom. Minor changes to the 2027 Technical Regulations were also agreed.

The changes are not final yet

All regulatory amendments remain subject to approval by the FIA World Motor Sport Council. The direction for 2027 is clear, but Paddock Command will treat the package as Commission-approved until the formal regulatory process is completed.

How this fits Formula 1's 2027 reset

The shorter race distance is unlikely to transform Formula 1 by itself. Its significance comes from the accumulation of smaller effects: less fuel, slightly shorter tyre stints, narrower recovery windows and revised calculations when a Safety Car appears late.

Combine those changes with a 2027 calendar featuring 24 Grands Prix and 10 Sprint weekends, and Formula 1 is continuing to reshape both the quantity and structure of competitive running.

The calendar told us where Formula 1 will race in 2027. The latest Commission decisions are beginning to tell us how those races will work.
