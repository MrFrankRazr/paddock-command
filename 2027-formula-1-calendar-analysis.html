2027 F1 Calendar: 24 Races, 10 Sprints and Two Major Returns

Formula 1 has confirmed a 24-round 2027 championship with a record 10 Sprint weekends, Portugal and Türkiye returning, and Madring back for its second Formula 1 season.

Paddock Command editorial · Published Sep 16, 2026
Official 2027 calendar confirmed · World Motor Sport Council approved
GRANDS PRIX24
COUNTRIES22
SPRINTS10
PRE-SEASON TESTFeb 24–27

Formula 1 has confirmed the shape of its 2027 season.

The FIA and Formula 1 have announced a 24-round championship across 22 countries, with Portugal and Türkiye returning to the schedule and the Sprint format expanding from six weekends to 10.

The calendar has been approved by the FIA World Motor Sport Council and gives teams their first complete roadmap for the season following Formula 1's major 2026 technical reset.

For Paddock Command, three developments stand above the rest: a dramatically larger Sprint programme, the return of two established circuits, and another significant reshuffling of the championship's geographic sequence.

Bahrain takes back the season opener

After Formula 1's 2026 season began in Australia because Ramadan pushed the Bahrain and Saudi Arabian races into April, the Middle East returns to the front of the calendar in 2027.

Bahrain will host Formula 1's pre-season test from February 24–27 before opening the championship in March. Saudi Arabia follows immediately.

That gives teams an unusually concentrated start: four days of testing followed shortly afterward by the first competitive weekend at the same Bahrain International Circuit.

Australia moves to Round 3, followed by Japan and China. The championship then crosses the Pacific for Miami and Canada before beginning its European run.

Portugal is back

One of the most notable additions is Portimão. Formula 1 returns to the Autódromo Internacional do Algarve after the Portuguese circuit previously hosted Grands Prix in 2020 and 2021.

Its elevation changes and combination of blind crests, compression zones and technical corners produced a distinctive challenge during its previous Formula 1 appearances.

Portugal follows Monaco in the opening phase of the European season. Its return also gives Formula 1 another conventional permanent circuit at a time when an increasing share of the championship is contested at street or hybrid venues.

Türkiye returns as well

Istanbul Park also returns. The Turkish circuit last hosted Formula 1 in 2021 and has secured a five-year agreement extending through 2031.

Its defining feature remains Turn 8, the long multi-apex left-hander that became one of the circuit's signature technical challenges during Formula 1's previous visits.

Türkiye forms part of a sequence with Azerbaijan and Singapore, creating three consecutive events built around very different circuit characteristics: Baku's straight-line efficiency and braking demands, Istanbul Park's sustained corner loading, and Singapore's physically demanding street layout.

Madring gets its second Formula 1 weekend

Madrid remains firmly embedded in Formula 1's plans. Madring will stage its second Grand Prix after Monza in September.

The timing is significant after the inaugural 2026 race exposed weaknesses in the circuit's overtaking characteristics and prompted organisers to begin examining changes for 2027.

Teams will therefore return with a year of circuit data—but potentially to a modified version of the venue. Instead of simply refining setup knowledge from its debut, teams may have to combine that information with changes designed to improve racing.

Sprint racing expands dramatically

The biggest structural change is the Sprint programme. Formula 1 will increase Sprint weekends from six in 2026 to 10 in 2027.

Bahrain, Australia, Japan, Monaco and Abu Dhabi will host Sprints for the first time. Canada, Great Britain, Italy, Brazil and Qatar complete the 10-event programme.

That means more than 40% of the championship's Grand Prix weekends will use the Sprint format.

Sprint weekends reduce conventional practice time to a single session before competitive running begins. That places greater emphasis on simulation, baseline setup quality and rapid engineering decisions.

Monaco becomes particularly interesting

Of the new Sprint venues, Monaco stands out. The circuit already places extraordinary value on qualifying because overtaking during the Grand Prix is so difficult.

Adding a Sprint means drivers will face two qualifying sessions and two competitive starts during the weekend, with only one conventional practice session before the first competitive session.

At a circuit where confidence, track evolution and millimetre-level precision matter enormously, that could significantly increase the consequences of an imperfect Friday baseline.

Monza joins the Sprint schedule

Italy also receives another Sprint weekend. Monza's low-downforce configuration, extreme braking zones and slipstream effects create a very different Sprint problem from Monaco.

With limited practice, teams will need to establish energy management, aerodynamic efficiency and braking stability quickly. The Sprint also creates an additional competitive session immediately before the championship moves to Madrid.

A different competitive rhythm

Ten Sprint weekends change more than individual events. They alter the rhythm of an entire championship.

More competitive sessions mean more opportunities to score points, more starts, more exposure to incidents and fewer practice laps during a substantial portion of the season.

That increases the importance of operational consistency. Teams that repeatedly begin Sprint weekends inside the correct setup window can accumulate advantages before rivals have enough track time to understand why they are behind.

The calendar's geography changes again

The opening Middle Eastern double-header leads into an Australia-Japan-China sequence. Miami and Canada form the North American transition into Europe.

The European phase stretches through the summer before Monza and Madrid restart the championship after the break. Azerbaijan, Türkiye and Singapore then form another concentrated sequence.

Austin, Mexico City and São Paulo create the late-season Americas run before Las Vegas. Qatar and Abu Dhabi close the championship in December.

Formula 1 says the structure is intended to improve the geographic flow of freight and personnel while supporting its longer-term sustainability objectives.

2027 calendar signalTwenty-four Grands Prix remain the baseline, but a record 10 Sprint weekends reduce preparation time across more than 40% of the season. Portugal and Türkiye return, Madring enters year two, and the championship again places a premium on rapid adaptation between sharply different circuit types.

What the 2027 calendar tells us already

The calendar announcement does not tell us which team will be strongest when Formula 1 enters the second season of its current technical regulations. It does tell us what kind of championship they will have to navigate.

Portugal and Türkiye restore two demanding permanent circuits. Madring enters its second year with lessons from its difficult inaugural race. Several of Formula 1's most specialised venues will now operate under the compressed Sprint format.

The 2027 championship will therefore demand more than outright car performance. Rapid setup work, operational consistency and the ability to adapt quickly from one circuit type to another will carry even greater weight.

Formula 1 now has its roadmap. The engineering challenge begins long before Bahrain.
