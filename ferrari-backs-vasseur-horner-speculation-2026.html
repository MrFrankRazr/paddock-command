Ferrari Backs Vasseur as Team Moves to End Horner Speculation

Ferrari says it has full confidence in Fred Vasseur and wants to shift attention from leadership speculation to performance ahead of the return to Sepang.

Paddock Command editorial · September 29, 2026

Ferrari has publicly reaffirmed its support for Formula 1 team principal Fred Vasseur following renewed speculation linking former Red Bull boss Christian Horner with Maranello.

In a media briefing on September 29, Ferrari said it had already made its leadership choices and expressed full confidence in Vasseur and his team. The intervention came as the Scuderia prepared for this weekend’s Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia at Sepang.

Ferrari responds to leadership speculation

Horner's comments in a recent interview, in which he described Ferrari as a dream destination, renewed discussion about Vasseur's position. Ferrari's public response was explicit about its present leadership arrangements, while declining to engage further with speculation about external candidates.

Vasseur had expressed frustration during the Azerbaijan weekend that the recurring discussion was distracting from the team's work. Ferrari's latest statement emphasized unity and the need to concentrate on the racing programme rather than speculation ahead of Malaysia.

The on-track challenge remains

Ferrari heads to Sepang second in the Constructors’ Championship with 378 points, 160 behind Mercedes and 72 ahead of McLaren. In Azerbaijan, Charles Leclerc finished fourth and Lewis Hamilton sixth, extending Ferrari's run without a Grand Prix podium to five races.

Leclerc has called for upgrades to narrow the gap to rivals. Vasseur said on September 28 that further Ferrari developments are coming, without specifying the race at which they will arrive. His explanation identified straight-line performance and setup compromises among the team's recent challenges.

Ferrari's public leadership backing does not itself resolve those competitive issues. Sepang provides the next opportunity to assess its race pace, aerodynamic efficiency and ability to challenge the leading teams.

Sepang becomes the immediate focus

The Malaysian circuit's long straights, fast corners and demanding conditions will offer a fresh comparison after Baku. Ferrari's stated objective is to focus on the work ahead rather than allow the leadership discussion to dominate another race weekend.

Paddock Command watch: Friday practice should offer the first evidence of whether Ferrari's Baku straight-line limitations persist at Sepang. Watch Leclerc and Hamilton's speed-trap performance alongside their long-run consistency before drawing conclusions from a single qualifying lap.
