Mercedes Splits Singapore Car Specifications as Russell Warns of Another Engine Penalty

Mercedes will run two different aerodynamic specifications during the Singapore Grand Prix weekend as the team investigates its disappointing performance at Sepang.

Mercedes takes two different development paths

George Russell confirmed that he will return to the previous aerodynamic specification used during his Azerbaijan Grand Prix victory. Kimi Antonelli will continue with the updated package introduced at Sepang.

Antonelli's car will also feature a new front wing designed to complement the latest upgrade package. The split gives Mercedes an opportunity to compare two development directions across Marina Bay's very different low-speed and braking-heavy layout.

The comparison matters because the latest package did not deliver the expected advantage in Malaysia. Mercedes now needs to understand whether the Sepang performance was circuit-specific or whether the newer specification still requires refinement.

Antonelli commits to the new package

Antonelli has committed to the latest specification for the full Singapore weekend rather than reverting to the previous configuration.

The additional front wing is significant because it was intended to work with the newer aerodynamic package. Singapore therefore gives Mercedes its first opportunity to evaluate the update with the complementary component fitted in race-weekend conditions.

The comparison will not be perfectly controlled. Driver differences, setup choices, track evolution and environmental conditions will all influence the data, but the split still gives Mercedes valuable directional information.

Russell expects another engine penalty

Russell's Singapore weekend is already compromised by the power-unit failure that ended his Sepang race. Mercedes requires fresh hardware, and Russell will start Sunday's Grand Prix from the back of the grid.

Russell has now said he expects another engine penalty before the 2026 season concludes because of the mileage demands on his remaining power-unit pool.

No additional future penalty has yet been imposed. The statement is an expectation rather than an official sanction, but it adds another complication to an already difficult championship position.

The Sprint remains Russell's immediate opportunity

The Sunday grid sanction does not apply to Saturday's Sprint. Russell therefore has a separate opportunity to score before the Grand Prix recovery drive begins.

That makes Friday's only practice session especially important. Russell must establish a workable baseline with the older aerodynamic package while Antonelli evaluates the newer specification and front wing before Sprint Qualifying.

Championship pressure continues to increase

Antonelli enters Singapore leading the Drivers' Championship with 320 points. Russell is second on 236, leaving an 84-point deficit. Lewis Hamilton remains third on 214.

The gap widened after Russell's Sepang retirement and Antonelli's second-place finish. Singapore now combines championship pressure with an unusually important technical comparison inside Mercedes.

Environmental and operational conditions add complexity

Mercedes' evaluation will take place during a weekend already carrying significant operational demands.

The FIA has activated heat-hazard regulations for Saturday's Sprint and Sunday's Grand Prix. Singapore's unhealthy haze remains under observation, while the FIA has also confirmed a wet-weather software contingency following the Sepang power-unit software investigation.

The published racing timetable remains unchanged.

Paddock Command verdict

Mercedes enters Singapore with two linked but distinct objectives: establish whether its latest aerodynamic package offers a meaningful performance gain and limit the championship damage created by Russell's power-unit situation.

Russell's return to the Baku-winning configuration provides familiarity, while Antonelli's updated car gives Mercedes a direct opportunity to validate its latest development path.

The first meaningful answers will arrive in Friday practice. For Russell, the immediate target is to maximize the Sprint before Sunday's recovery drive. For Antonelli, the opportunity is to strengthen both Mercedes' technical understanding and his championship position.
