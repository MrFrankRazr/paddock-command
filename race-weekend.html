RED BULL WATCH · SEP 23

Red Bull Locks Hadjar In for 2027 as He Returns in Baku

Red Bull has removed two uncertainties surrounding Isack Hadjar in the space of 24 hours: he is returning to the RB22 in Baku, and his place alongside Max Verstappen is now secured for 2027.

2027Hadjar confirmed
TEAM-MATEMax Verstappen
ABSENCEThree Grands Prix
QUALIFYINGP4 · Hadjar

The team announced Hadjar's contract extension on Wednesday, completing its 2027 driver line-up. Verstappen is already committed to Red Bull through the end of 2030.

Hadjar returns to competition after missing Zandvoort, Monza and Madrid while recovering from his wrist injury. His pre-injury form was a major factor in Red Bull's decision: the team highlights seven consecutive top-six finishes and his Monaco podium among the strongest markers of his 2026 campaign.

The immediate focus now shifts from contract certainty back to physical performance. Baku's heavy braking, rapid direction changes and unforgiving street-circuit layout make Thursday's two practice sessions a demanding first test after three Grands Prix away.

Paddock Command watch: Hadjar no longer has a 2027 contract question hanging over his comeback. Thursday is about whether he can immediately regain rhythm, complete representative long runs and handle a full practice workload comfortably.

MCLAREN UPGRADE WATCH · SEP 23

McLaren Brings Major Upgrade Package to Baku

McLaren has added another major technical storyline to the Azerbaijan Grand Prix, arriving in Baku with what is expected to be its most significant development package of the second half of 2026.

PACKAGENew floor + aerodynamic revisions
H-WINGRotating low-drag rear wing returns
KEY TESTThursday FP1 + FP2 evaluation
BAKU TARGETEfficiency without sacrificing stability

The MCL40 changes include a new floor and further aerodynamic revisions, while McLaren is also bringing back its rotating H-Wing rear-wing system after leaving the low-drag concept off the car in Madrid.

The H-Wing has already run competitively at Monza and is designed to reduce drag when straight-line efficiency becomes critical. Baku should provide an especially revealing test because the circuit combines an exceptionally long full-throttle section with heavy braking and slower technical corners.

The floor development may ultimately be the more important component. If it delivers additional aerodynamic load without sacrificing the efficiency McLaren needs down Baku's long straight, the package could materially alter the competitive order at the front.

Paddock Command watch: Thursday's FP1 and FP2 speed traps, sector performance and long-run consistency should reveal whether McLaren has gained only straight-line efficiency or made a broader step with the MCL40.

TYRE INTELLIGENCE · SEP 22

Baku Points Toward a One-Stop Race

Pirelli has selected the softest range of compounds for Azerbaijan: C3 Hard, C4 Medium and C5 Soft.

HARDC3
MEDIUMC4
SOFTC5
BASELINEOne-stop strategy expected

Sections around Turns 2, 3 and 4 have been resurfaced, but Pirelli does not expect the localized new asphalt to materially change tyre behaviour. The larger factor should be track evolution as rubber builds through the weekend.

Baku's smooth surface is expected to produce very low wear and degradation, making a one-stop strategy the likely baseline. The long full-throttle run toward Turn 1 can also cool the tyres substantially before one of the circuit's heaviest braking zones, increasing the risk of front locking.

Strategy watch: The C5 soft could prove capable of surprisingly long stints, while the C3 hard may see limited race use.

BAKU WEATHER WATCH · SEP 22

Wind, Not Rain, Is the Early Baku Variable

The latest official Azerbaijan Grand Prix forecast continues to point toward a dry Baku, but strong winds could make the weekend considerably less straightforward.

Thursday is forecast to reach around 28°C with no rain expected and gusts potentially reaching 57.6 kph. Friday becomes the key weather session: FP3 and qualifying are forecast around 24°C with gusts potentially reaching 64.8 kph.

Saturday's Grand Prix remains dry in the current outlook, with temperatures around 26°C and gusts potentially reaching 43.2 kph.

FRI · FP3/QUALI24°C · 0% RAIN · GUSTS TO 64.8 KPH

At a circuit defined by heavy braking, exposed straights and close barriers, changing wind strength could influence braking stability and car positioning. Friday qualifying currently carries the strongest gust forecast of the weekend.

Paddock Command watch: Friday's wind remains the key variable. The forecast should be checked again before FP1 as conditions can still change before cars reach the circuit.

BAKU FAN TRAVEL · SEP 19

Azerbaijan Opens Visa on Arrival for F1 Visitors

Fans traveling internationally for the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix have a simplified entry option. Azerbaijan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs says foreigners and stateless persons arriving in connection with the Formula 1 event can obtain visas on arrival at the country’s international airports through September 30.

To qualify, travelers must present an accepted F1-related document: Formula One/FIA accreditation, Baku City Circuit accreditation, a Grand Prix ticket or e-ticket, or documentation confirming purchase of a race ticket.

The provision covers the entire Baku race period, including the Thursday-to-Saturday September 24–26 Grand Prix weekend.

Travel note: Entry requirements can depend on individual circumstances. Travelers should confirm their eligibility and documentation with Azerbaijan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs before departure.
