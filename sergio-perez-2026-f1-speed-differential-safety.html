A Baku near miss renews the debate

Sergio Pérez has renewed concerns about closing speeds in Formula 1's 2026 cars, saying he narrowly avoided a major collision with Liam Lawson while defending during the Azerbaijan Grand Prix. The Cadillac driver attributed the near miss to differences in electrical-energy deployment between their cars and called for changes to the regulations.

The incident Pérez described was a near miss, not a collision. His account adds a driver's first-hand perspective to a technical debate that has continued throughout the season.

Why deployment changes closing speeds

The 2026 power units rely far more heavily on electrical energy. Cars with different amounts of energy available—or different deployment strategies—can arrive at the same part of the circuit with substantially different speeds. A following driver may close on another car faster than expected, reducing the time available to react.

Pérez argues that the differences between manufacturers make the issue harder to manage. His concern is specifically about the speed difference when one car has strong deployment and another has much less available power; his comments do not establish that every such encounter is dangerous.

Japan provided an earlier warning

Closing-speed concerns became prominent after Oliver Bearman's Japanese Grand Prix crash while taking avoiding action around a slower Franco Colapinto. The FIA subsequently made changes during the April break intended to reduce the need for extreme lift-and-coast tactics. Pérez's Baku account indicates that drivers still want the remaining differences reviewed.

Why Sepang is the next test

Formula 1 heads to Sepang on October 2–4. Its long straights and fast corners put energy deployment and harvesting back in focus, but the effect of the 2026 cars at this circuit will only become clear when practice begins. Pérez's comments make closing-speed behaviour an additional technical watch point alongside tyre management and tropical conditions.

Paddock Command watch pointFollow whether drivers report large speed differences in practice, whether the FIA issues further guidance, and whether energy-management changes are proposed. No new rule change has been announced as a direct consequence of Pérez's latest comments.
