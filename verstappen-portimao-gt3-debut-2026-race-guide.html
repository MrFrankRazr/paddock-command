RACE DEBRIEF · OCTOBER 17, 2026 · REPORTED RESULT

Verstappen Racing P9 at Portimão After Penalty and Late Fightback

Max Verstappen, Ben Barnicoat and Chris Lulham are reported to have finished ninth in Saturday’s three-hour GT World Challenge Europe finale at Portimão. The #3 Mercedes-AMG fought into the top six before a drive-through penalty for contact involving Lulham dropped it down the order. Verstappen recovered from P24 during the closing stint. The organizer’s official online race classification was not yet populated when this update was prepared; the placing is independently reported, pending official confirmation.

From P13 on the grid to a reported P9 finish

The #3 Mercedes-AMG Team Verstappen Racing crew began the weekend 15th in provisional combined qualifying, improved to P14 after the #65 Ford Mustang lost its times, and was subsequently promoted to P13 on the starting grid after a separate penalty for the #51 Ferrari. Its original three-driver qualifying average, 1:42.624, did not change. The distinction matters: P14 was the final combined qualifying classification; P13 was the reported race starting position.

Barnicoat took the opening stint and moved forward in a busy field of more than 60 GT3 cars. Lulham then continued the recovery, bringing the Mercedes into the top six and within reach of a significantly stronger finish than its qualifying result suggested.

The collision and drive-through that changed the race

During the middle stint, Lulham made contact with Luca Stolz in the #48 Mercedes-AMG while attempting a pass. Stolz spun, and the #3 crew received a drive-through penalty. The penalty transformed a potential top-six challenge into a recovery drive and left Verstappen well outside the top ten when he took over.

Verstappen rejoined in P24 for the final portion of the race. He gained positions through traffic, with a late full-course yellow and safety-car period compressing the field. His closing sequence of overtakes brought the #3 car back to ninth in the race reports. The safety-car reset helped create opportunities, but the crew still had to execute the passes on track.

GetSpeed wins the season finale

The #17 Mercedes-AMG Team GetSpeed car shared by Maximilian Götz, Ralf Aron and Fabian Schiller took victory, according to multiple independent race reports. The #80 Lionspeed GP Porsche of Ricardo Feller, Thomas Preining and Bastian Buus finished second, with the pole-sitting #2 Boutsen VDS Porsche of Morris Schuring, Dorian Boccolacci and Alessio Picariello third.

That result gives Portimão two distinct Mercedes stories: a winning GetSpeed operation at the front and a Verstappen Racing recovery after an avoidable penalty. It also underscores how endurance results depend on the full three-driver lineup, not the headline driver alone.

Result status: reported, awaiting organizer classification

As of this October 17 publication preparation, RacingNews365, GPblog and GPFans independently reported the #3 car in P9. The official GT World Challenge Europe Portimão Main Race results page had not yet populated its classification. Paddock Command therefore identifies P9 as a reported result rather than an organizer-certified final classification. Any later stewards’ amendment should be reflected in this same article and its Android entry.

What this means before Austin

The GT3 race does not change Formula 1 championship points. After Singapore, Mercedes driver Kimi Antonelli leads George Russell 340–238, a 102-point advantage; Verstappen is fourth on 221. His consecutive Formula 1 victories at Sepang and Singapore remain the relevant F1 form indicator, while the Portimão drive offers a separate look at his traffic management and adaptability in shared-car endurance racing.

Formula 1 resumes at Circuit of the Americas for the United States Grand Prix on October 23–25. Austin is a conventional three-practice-session weekend. Paddock Command’s existing COTA circuit guide and Antonelli championship scenarios remain the primary Formula 1 briefings; this Portimão result should sit below them as a completed off-weekend motorsport story.

Paddock Command assessment

The headline is the recovery, not simply the finishing position. Verstappen Racing showed pace sufficient to move into the top six before the drive-through and recovered to a reported P9 after falling to P24.
